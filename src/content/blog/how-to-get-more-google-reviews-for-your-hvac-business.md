# How to Get More Google Reviews for Your HVAC Business

Google reviews are not decoration. For an HVAC business, they are one of the fastest ways to look busy, trusted, and worth calling.

The good news is you do not need a giant marketing budget. You need the right timing, a simple script, and a system that does not depend on someone remembering it at 4:45 p.m.

## How many Google reviews does an HVAC business need?

There is no magic number, but the target changes as the business grows. A new HVAC company should aim for 20 to 30 reviews to stop looking brand new. Once you get past 50, the profile starts looking established in most local markets.

Recency matters just as much as total count. A shop with 28 reviews from the last six months can look stronger than one with 140 reviews from 2019. Google and homeowners both like signs that the business is still active.

That matters in Pittsburgh and every other city with a crowded home services market. A company in the South Hills with fresh reviews can look better than a bigger shop with stale feedback from two winters ago.

The rating matters too, but not as much as people think. A 4.8 with 32 recent reviews is usually plenty strong. Chasing a perfect 5.0 is a bad hobby.

## When should you ask for a Google review?

Ask when the customer can still feel the win. That usually means right after a successful repair, replacement, or tune-up. The homeowner should still remember the leak, the dead blower, or the house that finally got warm again.

Do not wait until the next invoice cycle if the job went well. The longer you wait, the colder the memory gets. A happy customer on Tuesday turns into a confused customer on Friday.

Emergency calls are a little different. If you showed up for a flooded basement or no-heat call, wait until things calm down. Ask later that day or the next morning. Nobody wants to leave a review while they are still drying out the carpet.

The best moment is usually before you leave the driveway. The problem is solved, the customer is relieved, and the service is still fresh. That is when the ask feels natural instead of forced.

## What to say when you ask for a review

Keep the ask short. Long speeches make people nervous. A review request should sound like a normal part of the job, not a bonus round.

A simple script works:

"Glad we got that fixed. If you were happy with the work, would you mind leaving a quick Google review? I can text the link."

That script works because it is direct and easy to answer. It also gives the customer one clear next step, which is what most people need.

Here are three more versions you can use:

- "Thanks for having us out today. If the system is running the way you wanted, could you leave a Google review?"
- "I’m glad we got your heat back on. Would you mind sharing a quick review when you have a minute?"
- "If you were happy with the service, I can send the review link right now."

Do not ask for a "good review." That sounds sketchy. Do not ask for five stars only. And do not offer a discount or gift card in exchange. That can backfire fast.

## How to automate review requests without making a mess

The best review system is boring. That is a compliment. It should fire every time a job is marked complete, then send one text or email with the review link.

Most HVAC CRMs and texting tools can handle this. Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, and similar platforms usually let you trigger a message after the invoice goes out or the job is closed. That keeps the office from playing reminder roulette.

A basic setup usually costs $20 to $100 a month if it lives inside software you already use. Printed QR cards or leave-behind cards often cost less than $50 for a small batch. The expensive part is not the tool, it is the habit.

A good workflow looks like this:

1. Tech closes the job in the field.
2. Office confirms the customer was happy.
3. The system sends one text with the review link.
4. A single follow-up goes out 48 hours later if there is no response.

That is enough. More follow-ups usually start feeling needy.

You can also put the review QR code on invoices, thank-you cards, and leave-behind sheets. If the customer has to hunt for the link, they probably will not leave the review.

## What not to do with HVAC Google reviews

Do not buy reviews. Google is not stupid, and customers are not blind. A weird burst of fake five-star reviews looks fake because it is fake.

Do not ask every customer in the same robotic way. The emergency no-heat call and the easy maintenance visit are not the same situation. A little judgment goes a long way.

Do not ignore negative reviews either. Reply fast, stay calm, and offer to fix the problem offline. Public arguments make the business look small, even when the complaint is unfair.

A simple reply works better than a defensive novel:

"Sorry we missed the mark here. Please call the office so we can look into it and make it right."

That keeps the tone professional and shows future customers that someone is paying attention. Review responses are part of your sales process now. People read them.

## A simple 30-day plan for more HVAC reviews

You do not need a giant overhaul. You need a routine the whole team can follow.

### Week 1, set up the system

Pick one review link, one QR code, and one text template. Put them in the CRM, the invoice, and the office playbook. Every tech should know the script.

### Week 2, start asking after every good job

Focus on customers who were clearly happy. Repairs that solved the problem, maintenance visits that went smoothly, and installs that finished cleanly are all good candidates.

### Week 3, clean up the profile

Make sure the Google Business Profile has the right category, service area, phone number, and website. Reviews help more when the profile itself looks alive and accurate.

### Week 4, review the results

Count new reviews, read the comments, and look for patterns. If most mentions say "on time" or "professional," that is good news. Use those words on the website and in future review requests.

A shop that does this for 30 days usually sees momentum. Not magic, momentum. That is the part that actually pays the bills.

## Final thoughts

More Google reviews do not come from luck. They come from asking at the right time, making the link easy, and repeating the same process every week.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does an HVAC business need?
A new HVAC business should aim for 20 to 30 reviews first. That gets you past the "who are these guys" stage. After that, 50 or more reviews usually makes the business look established in most local markets.

### Should HVAC techs ask for reviews by text or email?
Text usually works better because people open it faster and can tap the link on their phone. Email is still useful as backup, especially for office follow-up. The best setup uses both, but text should go first.

### When is the best time to ask for a review after an HVAC repair?
Ask right after the job is done and the customer can feel the result. For emergency calls, wait until the customer has calmed down. A next-day ask is fine if the job was stressful.

### How often should an HVAC business post on Google Business Profile?
Once a week is a strong target for most HVAC companies. A job photo, short update, or seasonal tip is enough. Consistency matters more than volume.

### Can you ask for Google reviews after emergency HVAC service?
Yes, but timing matters. Do not ask while the homeowner is still stressed or angry. Wait until the situation is under control, then send the link later that day or the next morning.
