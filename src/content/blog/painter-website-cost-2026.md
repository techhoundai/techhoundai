# How Much Does a Painter Website Cost in 2026?

A painter’s website should do three jobs: make the business look legit, answer basic questions fast, and turn visits into estimates. Anything else is decoration, and decoration does not buy paint.

In 2026, most painter websites land in three price bands. DIY sites are cheap, template builds sit in the middle, and custom agency work costs more when the business needs real lead generation.

## What a painter website actually needs

A painting company website does not need ten bells and three whistles. It needs clear service pages, strong photos, proof of work, and a way to request a quote without hunting through menus like it is a scavenger hunt.

At minimum, a painter website should include:

- Home page with a clear service area
- Interior painting and exterior painting pages
- Residential and commercial services if both are offered
- Before and after gallery
- Reviews or testimonials
- Quote form and click-to-call button
- FAQ page
- Service area pages for nearby towns

For a crew in Pittsburgh, that might mean separate pages for places like Shadyside, Dormont, Cranberry, and Mount Lebanon. Local pages matter because people do not search for “painting company near planet Earth.” They search for a painter near them.

## What a painter website costs in 2026

### DIY website: $0 to $800 upfront

A DIY painter site is the cheapest route. Think Squarespace, Wix, WordPress with a basic theme, or a starter site built by the owner’s cousin who “knows computers.”

Typical costs look like this:

- Domain: about $15 to $30 per year
- Hosting or website plan: $20 to $80 per month
- Theme or template: $0 to $300
- Basic plugins or apps: $0 to $200 per year

DIY works when the goal is simple visibility. It does not work well when the owner wants the site to produce steady estimate requests without much hand-holding.

### Freelancer or template build: $1,500 to $5,000

This is the sweet spot for many small painting companies. A good freelancer or small studio can build a clean, mobile-friendly site with the pages a real business needs.

This range usually covers:

- Custom setup of a template
- Basic copywriting help
- Contact forms and map embeds
- Review integration
- Photo cleanup and layout work
- Core local SEO setup

For a lot of painting contractors, this is the best value. The site looks credible, loads fast, and does not cost as much as a truck wrap plus a bad logo.

### Custom agency build: $5,000 to $15,000+

A custom build makes sense when the business has multiple crews, several service lines, or a plan to grow across a larger metro area. It also makes sense when the website needs stronger SEO, content strategy, conversion tracking, or automation.

That price usually includes:

- Custom design
- Copywriting
- Professional photo selection or direction
- Service area strategy
- Lead tracking
- Speed optimization
- Maintenance guidance

This is the lane for a business that wants the website to act like a sales rep, not a brochure.

## Where the money goes

A painter website gets expensive when it includes things that actually take time. Design is only part of it. Content, photos, and setup usually eat a bigger share of the budget.

### Copywriting matters more than most owners think

Bad copy kills good design. A visitor needs to know what kind of painting is offered, where the company works, and why the crew is worth calling.

Expect copywriting to cost anywhere from $300 to $2,000, depending on how much is written from scratch. If the site has service pages for kitchens, cabinets, trim, exteriors, and commercial work, the number climbs.

### Photos are not optional

Painting is visual. Stock photos of smiling people holding rollers in spotless white rooms do not help much. Real before and after photos close more leads.

If a business needs photography or image cleanup, budget $500 to $3,000. The more jobs shown, the better. A handful of real project photos usually beats a giant gallery of generic “professional painter” shots.

### Quote forms and booking tools

A simple quote form is cheap. A smart one asks for project type, property type, timeline, zip code, and photos.

Expect setup costs from $0 to $500 for a basic form. More advanced scheduling or CRM integrations can add $200 to $1,500. That is often money well spent, because fewer bad leads reach the inbox.

### Local SEO setup

A painter website without local SEO is just an expensive business card. The foundation should include title tags, location pages, internal links, review markup, and a solid Google Business Profile strategy.

Basic local SEO setup often costs $500 to $2,500. Ongoing SEO support can run $500 to $2,000 per month, depending on how aggressive the market is.

## What painters should not pay for

Some website features look fancy and do almost nothing.

Skip these unless there is a real reason:

- Full-screen animations that slow the site down
- Huge one-page sites with no service detail
- Custom apps disguised as websites
- Chat widgets that never answer the actual question
- Stock photo galleries pretending to be a portfolio
- Overbuilt admin systems the owner will never use

A painter in Pittsburgh does not need a website that feels like a startup launch page. A company in the South Hills needs calls, quotes, and service area clarity. The pretty stuff can wait.

## How to keep the budget under control

The cleanest way to keep costs sane is to build in phases.

### Phase 1: launch the money pages

Start with the pages that help the business get found and get contacted:

- Home
- About
- Interior painting
- Exterior painting
- Contact
- Reviews
- Service areas

That is enough for a solid launch. It gets the business online without paying for extras that can be added later.

### Phase 2: add sales support

Once the site is working, add cabinet painting, deck staining, drywall repair, commercial painting, or whatever else the crew actually sells. Add FAQs and project galleries next.

### Phase 3: track what happens

If no one tracks calls, forms, and quote requests, nobody knows what the site is doing. That is how businesses end up paying for web work and guessing at the results.

A simple analytics setup and call tracking can save money fast. It tells the owner which pages bring in leads and which ones are just digital wallpaper.

## The real answer for most painting companies

For a small painting company, a good website usually costs $1,500 to $5,000 upfront, plus modest monthly maintenance. A more serious lead generation build can cost $5,000 to $15,000 or more.

If the goal is just to exist online, the budget can stay lower. If the goal is to win quote requests in a competitive market, the site needs better content, real photos, and local SEO.

The cheapest site is rarely the cheapest long term. The one that gets ignored by customers costs more than the one built right the first time.

## FAQ

### How much does a small painter website cost?
A small painter website usually costs $1,500 to $5,000 if it is built by a freelancer or small agency. DIY can be cheaper, but it usually takes more owner time and can look less polished. Custom builds cost more when the site needs stronger SEO or more lead tracking.

### What pages should a painting contractor website have?
At minimum, it should have a home page, services pages, an about page, a contact page, reviews, and service area pages. Interior and exterior painting pages are usually the most important. Add specialty pages later if the business offers cabinet painting, commercial work, or deck staining.

### Do painters need local SEO on their website?
Yes. Most painting jobs are local, so the website should help the business show up in nearby searches. Service area pages, review content, and a strong Google Business Profile all help.

### Should a painter website show pricing?
Usually yes, but not always with exact numbers. A price range, common project examples, or a “request a quote” path works better than hiding everything. People want a ballpark before they call.

### How long does it take to build a painter website?
A simple site can take one to three weeks. A custom site with content, SEO, and project photos can take four to eight weeks. The timeline depends on how fast the owner can approve content and gather photos.
