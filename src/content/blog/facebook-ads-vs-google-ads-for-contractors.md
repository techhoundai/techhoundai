# Facebook Ads vs Google Ads for Contractors: Which Gets Better Leads?

Facebook Ads and Google Ads both bring in leads, but they do it in very different ways. Google catches people who are already searching for help. Facebook shows up before they start searching. This guide breaks down which one gets better leads, what each one costs, and when a contractor should use both.

The short answer is simple. Google Ads usually wins for immediate jobs. Facebook Ads usually wins for cheaper reach and follow-up. If the goal is booked calls this week, start with Google. If the goal is awareness, remarketing, or seasonal offers, Facebook earns a spot.

## Facebook Ads vs Google Ads for contractors

Google Ads works when someone types a problem into the search bar. They need an electrician, a plumber, a roofer, or an HVAC tech now. That makes the traffic expensive, but the intent is strong.

Facebook Ads works when a homeowner sees an offer while scrolling. They might not need service today. They may need a reminder, a maintenance offer, or proof that your crew does clean work.

That difference matters. Google is a pull channel. Facebook is a push channel. One catches demand, the other creates it.

## How Google Ads work for contractors

Google Ads is usually the better choice for emergency and high-intent jobs. Think burst pipe, AC not cooling, panel upgrade, roof leak, or sewer backup. Those searches are the digital version of someone waving their arms in the driveway.

A good Google campaign should point to one service, one area, and one clear call to action. Do not send paid clicks to the homepage and hope for the best. That is how money disappears quietly.

A strong setup usually includes:

- One landing page for each main service
- Call tracking so you know what produced the lead
- Location targeting around your service area
- Call extensions and form fills
- Negative keywords to block junk searches

For many local contractors, Google Ads leads land somewhere around $40 to $150 each. In a competitive metro, they can go higher. The price hurts less when one booked job covers the month.

Google also tends to work best when the office answers fast. A contractor who calls back in five minutes usually beats one who waits until after lunch. Search intent fades fast. Homeowners do not sit there polishing their patience.

### When Google Ads wins

Google Ads usually wins when the service is urgent, expensive, or both. That includes plumbing repairs, HVAC replacements, electrical troubleshooting, and storm damage roofing.

It also wins when the business has a tight service area. A contractor in Pittsburgh, for example, can target the South Hills, Cranberry, Monroeville, or specific ZIP codes without wasting spend on the wrong side of town.

If the job is worth $800 or more, Google is often the better first test. The higher the ticket, the easier it is to justify the click price.

## How Facebook Ads work for contractors

Facebook Ads is better at starting interest than catching urgency. It is good for before-and-after photos, seasonal promotions, review requests, financing offers, and maintenance reminders.

That does not mean Facebook leads are bad. It means they are colder. People often fill out a form because the ad was interesting, not because their furnace died ten minutes ago.

That is why follow-up matters so much. A Facebook lead that gets a fast text and a phone call can turn into a real job. A Facebook lead that sits for six hours usually turns into a shrug.

A good Facebook campaign often uses:

- A simple offer, like a tune-up or inspection
- Strong photos of real work, not stock images
- A short form with only a few fields
- Retargeting for website visitors and past customers
- Seasonal messages that match the weather

Facebook leads often cost less than Google leads. It is common to see lower cost per lead, sometimes in the $10 to $50 range. The catch is quality. Cheap leads are not a win if half of them never answer the phone.

### When Facebook Ads wins

Facebook Ads works well for services people plan ahead of time. That includes painting, landscaping, deck work, pressure washing, gutter cleaning, and routine HVAC maintenance.

It also works well for remarketing. If someone visited the website, clicked around, and left without calling, Facebook can keep the business in front of them. That is useful because most homeowners do not book on the first visit.

For local contractors, Facebook is also handy when the brand needs more visibility. A new shop in Pittsburgh will not get remembered by magic. It has to show up a few times before the phone rings.

## Which platform gets better leads for plumbers, HVAC techs, roofers, and electricians

For emergency and repair work, Google usually gets better leads. A plumber with a backed-up sewer line is not browsing Facebook for inspiration. They are searching for the first company that answers the phone.

For replacement and planned work, both platforms can help. A roofer can use Google to catch storm-related searches and Facebook to show finished jobs, financing options, and neighborhood proof.

Here is the rough rule by trade:

- **Plumbers:** Google first, Facebook second
- **HVAC techs:** Google first for repairs, Facebook for tune-ups and tune-up reminders
- **Electricians:** Google first for service calls, Facebook for panel upgrades and rewires
- **Roofers:** Google for urgent leaks, Facebook for photo proof and seasonal campaigns
- **Painters and landscapers:** Facebook can work well because the work is visual and planned ahead

If the service is urgent, Google usually wins. If the service is visual, seasonal, or planned weeks ahead, Facebook can pull its weight.

## How much contractors should spend to test both platforms

A small contractor does not need a giant budget to start. The trick is to spend enough to learn something.

A practical test budget looks like this:

- **Google Ads:** $600 to $1,500 per month for one service and one area
- **Facebook Ads:** $300 to $900 per month for one offer or one audience
- **Combined test:** $1,000 to $2,000 per month for 60 to 90 days

That is enough to see patterns without lighting money on fire. If a campaign gets clicks but no calls, the problem is often the landing page, the offer, or the follow-up. It is not always the ad platform.

Start with one primary service. Do not run ads for every trade under the sun. That is how the budget gets diluted and nobody can tell what worked.

### A simple split that works

For most contractors, a good starting split is 70 percent Google and 30 percent Facebook. Google handles the urgent demand. Facebook handles follow-up and awareness.

That split changes if the business sells more planned work than emergency work. A painting company may lean harder into Facebook. A drain cleaning company should lean harder into Google.

## A simple ad plan for a local trade business

Keep the plan boring. Boring plans usually make more money.

### Step 1: Build one page for one service

Do not send ads to the homepage. Build a page for the job people actually want. If the ad is about water heater repair, the page should talk about water heater repair.

### Step 2: Make the phone number easy to find

Put the number at the top of the page and on mobile sticky call buttons. A lot of trade leads happen on a phone, not a laptop.

### Step 3: Track every call and form fill

Without tracking, the ad budget turns into a guess. Use call tracking, form tracking, and monthly review notes. Otherwise the office will swear the ads are working, but nobody will know which ones.

### Step 4: Follow up fast

The best ads in the world cannot save slow follow-up. Answer calls, text missed calls, and reply to form fills quickly. A five-minute response window beats a perfect headline.

### Step 5: Review the numbers every week

Look at cost per lead, booked jobs, and average job value. Do not obsess over clicks. Clicks do not pay the utility bill.

## Final thoughts

Facebook Ads and Google Ads both have a place in contractor marketing. Google is usually the better choice for urgent jobs and high-intent searches. Facebook is usually better for awareness, remarketing, and planned work.

If the business only has budget for one platform, start with Google. If the phone is already ringing and the shop wants more repeat exposure, add Facebook next.

For contractors in Pittsburgh and beyond, the winner is not the platform with the coolest dashboard. It is the one that gets the right lead to call back and book.
