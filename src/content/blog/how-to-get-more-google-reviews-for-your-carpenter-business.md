# How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Carpenter Business in 2026

Carpenter businesses win jobs on trust. Google reviews give that trust a name, a star rating, and a few sentences from real customers.

The fastest way to get more Google reviews for your carpenter business is simple: ask right after a clean job, send one review link by text, and follow up once if they forget. A small carpentry shop with 20 to 30 recent reviews usually looks established in most local markets. Fresh reviews matter more than a giant pile of old ones.

## Why Google reviews matter for carpenters

Homeowners do not know how to judge crown molding, cabinet fit, stair trim, or deck framing. They judge the finish, the communication, and whether another homeowner would hire you again.

Google reviews help in three ways. They improve click-through rate from the map pack, they make your business look safer to hire, and they give customers proof that you show up and finish clean.

A profile with recent reviews also makes your photos hit harder. A finished basement with five strong reviews looks much better than the same photo on a dead profile from 2022.

## How many Google reviews does a carpenter business need?

There is no magic number, but there is a practical floor.

- 0 to 5 reviews: new and hard to trust.
- 10 to 15 reviews: enough to stop looking blank.
- 20 to 30 reviews: established for a small local carpenter.
- 50 reviews or more: strong social proof in most towns.
- 100 reviews or more: now you look like a real local brand, not a side hustle.

In a market like Pittsburgh, 25 recent reviews can beat a prettier profile with 80 reviews that are mostly old. Recency matters because customers want proof that you are active now, not just that you were busy three years ago.

Ask for a steady trickle. Two reviews a week is better than 20 reviews in one weird burst and then silence.

## When to ask customers for a review

The best time is right after the customer has seen the finished work, checked the details, and paid the bill without complaints.

For carpenters, that means after the final walkthrough. It is even better if the customer says something like, "This looks great." That is your cue. Ask while the win is still warm.

### Do not ask too early

Do not ask in the middle of a cabinet install or before the punch list is done. People leave reviews based on how the whole job ended, not how good the first half looked.

If the project had a delay or a small mistake, fix it first. A happy customer who feels taken care of will usually leave a better review than someone you nagged during the messy part.

### The best channels are text and email

Text usually gets the most replies. Email is fine if your customers already check it, but text is faster and easier on a job site.

The goal is one tap, not a scavenger hunt. Every extra click kills your response rate.

## What to say when asking for a Google review

Keep it short. Do not sound like a robot reading from a sales brochure.

In person, try this:

"Thanks again for the work. If you were happy with the job, would you mind leaving a Google review? It helps a small carpentry business like ours a lot."

If they say yes, send the link right away. Do not tell them to "look it up later." Later means never.

A good text message looks like this:

"Thanks again for letting us handle your trim and built-ins. If you’ve got 30 seconds, would you mind leaving a Google review? Here’s the link."

That is it. No paragraphs. No guilt trip. No twelve-step explanation of how much the review helps the business.

### Ask for specifics, not fake praise

A good review mentions the job type, the city, and one thing the customer liked. That helps with trust and local SEO.

For example, "new basement stairs," "custom cabinets in Bethel Park," or "crown molding in Mt. Lebanon" all add useful detail. It sounds real because it is real.

## How to build a review system that runs every week

A review process should not depend on someone remembering it after a long day. That is how good intentions disappear.

Here is a simple setup:

1. Finish the job and send the invoice.
2. Send one thank-you text with the review link.
3. If there is no response after two days, send one reminder.
4. Reply to every review within a day.
5. Track which jobs got asked and which ones did not.

That system takes less than five minutes per job. The hard part is consistency, not complexity.

### Put one person in charge

Someone has to own the process. It can be the office manager, the owner, or whoever handles scheduling.

If everybody owns it, nobody does. That is how review requests get skipped for six months.

### Use a QR code on invoices or leave-behinds

A QR code is useful when customers prefer scanning instead of clicking a link. Put it on invoices, estimate sheets, or a small thank-you card left behind after the job.

It works best when the code goes to one place only, your Google review link. Do not send people to a page full of buttons and distractions.

### Automate the reminder if you can

If you already use Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or a simple CRM, set the review request to go out automatically after job completion. That keeps the process from depending on memory.

Most small setups are cheap. A basic reminder system can cost less than one canceled estimate, and it saves a lot of awkward follow-up.

## What to do when a bad review shows up

Do not panic. Do not argue in public. Do not write a defensive novel in the reply.

Reply quickly, stay calm, and acknowledge the issue. If the complaint is real, say what you fixed or how you plan to make it right. If it is fake or clearly not your customer, report it once and move on.

A simple reply works better than a dramatic one:

"Sorry to hear this. That is not the experience we want for our customers. Please call or email us so we can look into it and fix what we can."

That answer does three things. It shows future customers you care, it keeps the conversation professional, and it avoids turning one bad review into a bigger mess.

## A 30-day plan for a carpenter business

You do not need a six-month marketing project. You need a clean habit and a little follow-through.

### Week 1: clean up your Google Business Profile

Add your best job photos, service areas, hours, and a real website link. Make sure the category matches what you actually do.

If you do trim, cabinets, decks, finish carpentry, or framing, list the services clearly. Customers should not have to guess.

### Week 2: write your review script

Pick one text message and one in-person line. Use the same wording every time.

That keeps the process easy for your crew and consistent for customers. Consistency wins here.

### Week 3: ask every happy customer

Do not wait for the perfect job or the perfect mood. Ask the customers who were happy this week.

Five asks should turn into one or two reviews if your timing is decent. That is enough to keep momentum going.

### Week 4: check your results

Look at how many jobs got asked, how many replies came back, and how many reviews were posted. If the numbers are low, the problem is usually timing or follow-up.

Fix the process before you blame the customer. Most of the time, the customer never got asked clearly enough.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a carpenter business need?

There is no exact number, but 20 to 30 recent reviews usually makes a small carpenter business look established. Fifty or more starts to feel strong in most local markets. Freshness matters, so a steady flow beats a giant pile of old reviews.

### When is the best time to ask for a Google review?

Ask after the final walkthrough, when the customer has seen the finished work and feels good about it. That is usually right after the job is complete and the invoice is sent. Do not ask in the middle of a messy project or before the punch list is done.

### Should carpenters ask for reviews by text or email?

Text usually works better because people read it faster and can tap the link on their phone. Email still works, especially if that is how the customer already communicates with you. If you only pick one, pick text.

### What should a carpenter do after a negative Google review?

Reply calmly, thank the customer for the feedback, and offer to fix the issue offline. Do not get defensive or start a public fight. A professional reply matters because future customers will read it too.

### Can you ask for Google reviews after a cabinet or trim job?

Yes, and that is often the best time because the customer can see the finished work right away. Ask after the final cleanup and walkthrough, not before. If they love the result, they are much more likely to leave a detailed review.
