Most HVAC businesses should post on Google Business Profile once a week. During peak heating and cooling season, twice a week is fine if the posts are real updates, offers, job photos, or service reminders.

Google does not publish a required posting cadence, and posting more does not magically push you up in Maps. The point is to stay active, show real work, and give shoppers one more reason to call your shop instead of the guy three listings below you.

## How often should an HVAC business post on Google Business Profile?

The short answer is once a week. That pace is enough for most HVAC shops to look active without turning every post into filler.

If you are in a busy market, or you run strong seasonal campaigns, go up to two posts per week. That works well for spring AC tune-ups, summer no-cooling calls, fall furnace checks, and winter emergency heat repairs.

If you are a smaller shop with one truck and a packed schedule, four solid posts a month is a good floor. One good post beats ten awkward ones that read like they were written by a haunted printer.

A simple cadence looks like this:

- **Slow months:** 2 posts per month
- **Normal months:** 1 post per week
- **Peak season:** 2 posts per week

That schedule gives you enough activity to stay visible without wasting time. It also keeps your profile from looking frozen the same way a dead furnace looks in January.

## Do Google Business Profile posts help HVAC SEO?

Not directly, at least not in the way most owners hope. Google has said local ranking depends on relevance, distance, and prominence, and industry studies show post quantity itself is not a ranking factor.

That does not mean posts are useless. They help with clicks, calls, and trust, which is what matters once someone finds your profile.

A good post can do three jobs at once. It can prove you are active, show a real job or offer, and move a customer toward booking.

Think of GBP posts as conversion content, not magic ranking fuel. Your HVAC profile still needs a strong primary category, complete service info, good photos, real reviews, and a website that does not look abandoned.

## What should an HVAC company post on Google Business Profile?

Post things that help a homeowner decide to call now. That means seasonal service reminders, short offers, project photos, maintenance tips, and emergency repair reminders.

The best posts are specific. "Now booking AC tune-ups in Pittsburgh" beats "We are open and ready to help." One sounds like a business. The other sounds like a refrigerator magnet.

Good post ideas for HVAC shops:

- Spring AC tune-up reminders
- Fall furnace inspection offers
- Before-and-after system replacement photos
- Filter change tips for homeowners
- Financing reminders for full system installs
- Same-day service availability during heat waves
- Indoor air quality or humidifier education
- Maintenance plan promotions

Each post should have one job. Pick one service, one local angle, and one call to action. If the post needs a map to understand it, it is too busy.

A decent format is simple:

1. State the service.
2. Add one local detail.
3. Tell people what to do next.

Example: "Need a furnace tune-up before the first cold snap? Book a checkup in Pittsburgh and avoid a no-heat call in the middle of dinner." That is clear, local, and useful.

## Best Google Business Profile posting schedule for HVAC by season

HVAC marketing should follow the weather, because your customers do. Nobody in July wants a long story about winter prep, and nobody in February cares about summer AC coils.

### Spring

Spring is when the AC money starts moving. Post about tune-ups, cleanings, system checks, and replacement quotes.

This is a good time for one post each week. If you run a maintenance plan, post one reminder and one benefit post each month.

### Summer

Summer is the busiest time for most HVAC companies. Post about emergency cooling, same-day repairs, thermostat issues, and weak airflow.

If your team can handle it, two posts per week is smart here. People are in pain, and pain makes them click faster.

### Fall

Fall is furnace season. Post about heating inspections, ignition issues, dirty filters, and weird smells when the heat kicks on.

This is also the right time to talk about maintenance agreements. A simple reminder can keep a customer from calling the competition later.

### Winter

Winter is great for no-heat calls, indoor air quality, humidifiers, and emergency service. Post about common furnace problems and what to do before the cold gets worse.

If the phones are slammed, keep the pace to once a week. Good winter posts do not need to be fancy. They just need to be clear and timely.

## What makes a good HVAC Google Business Profile post?

A good post is short, specific, and easy to act on. It should look like it came from a real shop that actually works in the field.

Use a real photo whenever you can. A furnace replacement in a basement, a clean AC install, or a tech on a roof is better than a stock image of a smiling guy in a polo.

Keep these rules in mind:

- Use one service per post
- Add one local detail, like Pittsburgh, McKeesport, or Cranberry
- Keep the copy under 150 words when possible
- Add a button that matches the goal, like Call now or Learn more
- Use the same tone your best tech would use when explaining a repair to a homeowner

If you run multiple locations, make each post local. Do not copy the same post into every city and call it strategy. Customers can smell copy-paste from a mile away.

Google also archives old posts after six months unless they have a date range. That means your profile needs fresh updates, not one great post you wrote last year and forgot about.

## Common Google Business Profile posting mistakes HVAC contractors make

The biggest mistake is posting random junk just to stay active. A bland post with no photo and no CTA does not help anyone.

Another mistake is treating GBP like Facebook. Google Business Profile posts are not for rambling, jokes, or life updates from the shop dog. They are for local intent and booking.

Watch out for these problems:

- Copying social media captions word for word
- Posting the same offer for months
- Using vague text like "We are here for you"
- Skipping photos and buttons
- Writing posts that mention too many services at once
- Ignoring slow seasons until calls dry up

Also, do not post just to post. Four useful posts a month are better than twelve that say nothing. Search engines are not impressed by noise.

## Simple HVAC Google Business Profile posting system

The easiest system is a monthly batch. Pick four topics, grab four photos, and schedule the posts before the month starts.

A one-hour setup can handle most of it:

1. Pick one spring, summer, fall, or winter topic.
2. Pull a real job photo from the field.
3. Write 80 to 120 words.
4. Add one clear CTA.
5. Schedule the post and move on.

If you want more reach, build posts around real customer questions. "Why is my AC blowing warm air?" and "Why does my furnace smell when it starts?" are better post ideas than generic company updates.

That kind of content does two things. It helps the homeowner, and it gives your profile a reason to exist beyond your phone number.

## Final answer: how often should an HVAC business post on Google Business Profile?

Once a week is the right target for most HVAC businesses. In busy seasons, twice a week is even better if the content is local, useful, and tied to a real service.

Do not chase volume for its own sake. Chase useful posts, good photos, and a steady rhythm that matches how your shop actually works.
