# How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Landscaper Business

Google reviews help a landscaper business win trust fast. They also help you show up better on Google Maps, especially when nearby competitors have weak profiles or stale reviews. The good news is that most crews do not need a fancy system. They need a repeatable ask, the right timing, and a link that is easy to tap.

For most landscapers, the best results come from asking right after a job is finished, then following up once if the customer does not respond. A strong review flow can turn completed jobs into steady local proof. In Pittsburgh, that matters in places like the North Hills, South Hills, Cranberry, and Mt. Lebanon, where homeowners compare a few names and pick fast.

## Why Google reviews matter for landscapers

Google reviews do three jobs at once. They help with local SEO, they build trust, and they make your business look active. A profile with 12 recent reviews usually looks better than a profile with 80 old ones and no new activity.

Here is the short version: a landscaper with more recent, detailed reviews usually gets more calls than one with a bigger website and no proof.

That is especially true for services people do not buy every week. A homeowner may only hire for mulch, mowing, cleanup, planting, or weekly maintenance once in a while. When they search, they want a company that looks busy and reliable.

## How to get more Google reviews for your landscaper business

The best review request is short, specific, and sent while the job is still fresh in the customer’s head. Do not wait a month. By then, they have forgotten how good the cleanup looked.

### Ask after the final walkthrough

The best time is right after the customer sees the finished work. That could be after a spring cleanup, a mulch install, or a full yard refresh. If the customer is smiling and checking out the work, that is your moment.

Ask before you pull away from the driveway, or send the request within an hour. The closer the ask is to the job, the better the response rate.

### Ask after a visible win

Visible jobs get better reviews than invisible ones. A mowing visit is fine. A before-and-after mulch job is better. A yard cleanup, shrub trim, or sod install gives the customer something easy to mention in the review.

That matters because detailed reviews help more than one-line praise. "Great company" is nice. "They cleaned up the back yard, edged the beds, and showed up on time" is better for search and trust.

## Make the review request easy

Most customers want to help. They just do not want homework.

Give them one link. Better yet, give them one link and a QR code. Put it on invoices, estimate sheets, thank-you cards, and truck magnets if that fits your brand. If they have to search for your business again, you already lost half the battle.

### Text message template for landscapers

A short text works better than a long email. Keep it natural.

```text
Hi [Name], thanks again for letting us handle the [job type] at your property. If you were happy with the work, would you mind leaving a Google review? It helps a lot. Here is the link: [review link]
```

That message works because it names the job. It also gives a clear next step.

### Email template for landscapers

Email can work after bigger jobs, especially when the invoice is already going out.

```text
Subject: Thanks for the yard work

Hi [Name],

Thanks for choosing us for your [job type]. If you were happy with the service, would you leave a Google review? It helps local homeowners find a crew they can trust.

Review link: [link]

Thanks again,
[Business name]
```

Keep it short. Nobody wants a novel after paying for mulch.

## Build a repeatable review system

One-off asks are fine. A system is better.

Start by tagging happy customers in your CRM or spreadsheet. Every time a job closes, send the review request automatically. If the customer does not respond in two or three days, send one follow-up. That is enough.

A good system for a small crew looks like this:

- Job finishes.
- Office or crew lead sends the review link.
- Two days later, a reminder goes out.
- New reviews get answered within 48 hours.

That last step matters. Replying to reviews shows activity and gives you another chance to include service terms naturally. For example, "Thanks for trusting us with your spring cleanup" is better than a bland one-line reply.

If you run seasonal work, add review requests to your busiest jobs. Spring cleanups, fall leaf removal, mulch installs, and planting jobs usually generate stronger responses than routine mowing.

## How many Google reviews does a landscaper business need?

There is no magic number. But there are useful benchmarks.

For a small local crew, 15 to 25 recent reviews can look solid. Around 50 reviews starts to look established. If you are in a busy suburb with a lot of competitors, 75 to 100 reviews gives you more breathing room.

The real key is recency. Ten reviews from this month can help more than fifty reviews from three years ago. A steady flow tells Google and customers that the business is still active.

A practical target is 2 to 4 new reviews per month. If you do heavier seasonal work, aim higher during spring and fall. That pace is realistic for a working crew.

## What not to do when asking for reviews

Do not offer gift cards, discounts, or cash for reviews. That is messy, and it can get reviews removed.

Do not ask only your friends and family. That looks fake because it is fake. Real job reviews from real customers win every time.

Do not dump customers onto your homepage and hope they figure it out. Send the direct Google review link.

Do not ignore bad reviews either. Reply calmly, keep it short, and stay professional. A measured response often helps more than a defensive one.

## A simple 30-day review plan for landscapers

If the review count is low, fix it with a short sprint.

Week 1, build your direct Google review link and a QR code. Add both to invoices and estimate templates.

Week 2, text every happy customer from the last 30 days. Start with the best jobs first.

Week 3, set up one follow-up reminder for anyone who did not reply.

Week 4, reply to every new review and track which jobs got the best responses.

That is enough to start seeing steady growth. No fancy marketing circus required.

## Final thought

More Google reviews do not just make a landscaper business look better. They make it easier for a homeowner to trust you, call you, and book the job. Keep the ask short, make it easy, and ask when the work is fresh.
