# How Often Should a Plumber Post on Google Business Profile in 2026?

Google Business Profile posts are cheap visibility. For most plumbers, one solid post a week is enough. Add job photos more often if the work is visual, like water heater swaps, drain cleanings, or a nasty sewer backup nobody wants to think about twice.

The short answer is simple: post weekly, keep it useful, and do not treat your profile like a junk drawer. A few good updates beat ten lazy ones every time. Google is trying to help customers decide who to call, and stale profiles do not help that cause.

## How often should a plumber post on Google Business Profile?

Post once a week if you want a schedule that is easy to keep and strong enough to matter. That gives you 4 to 5 posts a month, which is plenty for most plumbing shops.

If you run a bigger crew or serve a few cities around Pittsburgh, aim for 2 posts a week. More jobs mean more photos, more seasonal offers, and more chances to show real work instead of stock images that look like they came from a bathroom showroom.

### A good posting rhythm for plumbers

- **1 weekly update post** about a service, project, or seasonal issue.
- **2 to 4 photo uploads per week** if you have fresh before-and-after work.
- **1 offer or promotion every 2 to 4 weeks** if you run specials.
- **Extra posts during peak season** for frozen pipes, sump pumps, water heater failures, or backflow testing.

That schedule is realistic for a busy plumber. It also keeps your profile looking active without turning your marketing into a second full-time job.

## What to post on Google Business Profile as a plumber

The best posts answer one of three questions: what do you do, what problem do you solve, and why should someone trust you. That is it. Nobody needs your life story, and nobody wants a paragraph about “exciting plumbing opportunities.”

### Use these post ideas

- Before-and-after drain cleaning photos.
- Water heater replacement photos with the model and fuel type.
- Seasonal reminders, like frozen pipe prep in winter.
- Sump pump checks before heavy rain.
- Sewer line inspection tips.
- Backflow testing reminders.
- Service area updates for neighborhoods like the South Hills, North Hills, or Cranberry.
- Short offers, like a discount on water heater flushes.

Keep the copy plain. Example: “Installed a new 50-gallon gas water heater in Bethel Park today. Customer had no hot water and got same-day service.” That is real, local, and useful.

### What not to post

Do not post empty fluff like “Happy Monday from our team!” unless there is a reason. Do not upload the same photo three times with different captions. Do not stuff every post with the same five keywords like a robot with a hammer.

Google wants professional, useful content. Your customers do too.

## Does posting on Google Business Profile help plumbers rank better?

Posting is not a magic button that jumps you to the top of Google Maps. Anyone selling that is trying to separate you from your money.

What posting does is keep your profile active and give customers more reasons to choose you. Google says posts can help share up-to-date info, promote offers, and connect customers with photos and videos. That matters when someone is comparing three plumbers and your profile looks alive while the other two look abandoned.

Here is the practical version: posts help with trust, clicks, and calls. That can improve how people interact with your profile, which is the part most owners actually care about.

For a plumber in Pittsburgh, that can mean a post about frozen pipe repairs in January, then another about sewer line inspections after the spring rain starts showing up. Same business, different season, better timing.

## How to make Google Business Profile posts worth your time

A good post takes 5 to 10 minutes if you already have the photo. The trick is to post something that sounds like a real job, not a marketing committee meeting.

### Use this simple format

1. State the problem.
2. Say what was done.
3. Add one local detail.
4. Finish with a call to action.

Example:
“Customer in Mt. Lebanon had a leaking shutoff valve under the kitchen sink. We replaced the valve, checked for hidden damage, and got the cabinet dry again. Call for same-day plumbing help.”

That works because it feels specific. It also gives Google and customers a clear topic.

### Best types of images

Photos matter more than most plumbers think. A real job site photo beats a polished stock image every time.

Use:

- Clean before-and-after shots.
- Equipment photos with the actual job in the background.
- Team photos on the truck if the crew is presentable.
- Short videos of repairs, leaks, or installations.

Skip blurry photos, dark basement shots, and anything that makes your company look like it borrowed a phone from 2014.

## Google Business Profile post rules plumbers need to know

Google reviews posts before they go live. That means ugly spam, junk text, and policy-breaking stuff can get blocked.

Google also says posts older than 6 months are archived unless they have a date range. So if you only post once a year, your profile will spend a lot of time looking empty.

A few other rules matter:

- Posts can include text, photos, videos, and action buttons.
- Offers, events, and updates work differently.
- Phone numbers in the post description can get rejected.
- You can schedule posts ahead of time.
- Some posts can be set to repeat on a weekly or monthly schedule.

That last part matters for busy plumbing shops. If winter is your heavy season, you can schedule a batch of frozen pipe and water heater posts before the first cold snap hits.

## A simple Google Business Profile schedule for a plumbing company

Here is a schedule that works for most plumbers without turning the office into a content factory.

### Solo plumber or small shop

- Monday: one project photo or service tip.
- Thursday: one seasonal post or trust post.
- Anytime: upload good job photos after a call.

### Growing plumbing company with a few techs

- Two posts a week.
- One customer education post.
- One proof post with photos from a real job.
- One offer each month, if you have one.

### Larger plumbing company with multiple service areas

- Two to three posts a week.
- Rotate by city or service area.
- Mix water heater, drain, sewer, leak detection, and emergency posts.
- Use seasonal campaigns before summer storms and winter freeze-ups.

The goal is not volume. The goal is enough consistency that people see your business as active and local.

## What to measure after you start posting

Do not obsess over likes. This is not a bakery page.

Watch these numbers instead:

- Calls from your Business Profile.
- Website clicks.
- Direction requests.
- Which posts get the most views.
- Which services keep showing up in customer conversations.

If water heater posts get more clicks than drain cleaning posts, make more water heater content. If emergency repair posts get the most calls, that is your answer. Let the data do the arguing for you.

## Final answer: how often should a plumber post?

For most plumbers, once a week is the sweet spot. It is enough to keep your Google Business Profile fresh, it is manageable, and it gives you a steady reason to post real jobs, real photos, and real offers.

If you already have a steady stream of before-and-after photos, twice a week is better. If you only post when you remember, your profile will look forgotten, which is not a great look when someone needs help now.
