# How to Get Your First 50 Google Reviews as a Contractor

The fastest way to get your first 50 Google reviews as a contractor is simple: ask every happy customer, send one direct review link, and follow up once. Most crews miss reviews because they wait too long and make the customer hunt for the profile.

A solid process can get a plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing, or painting company from zero to 50 reviews in a few months. The trick is consistency, not begging.

**Best answer:** ask within 24 hours of the job, text the review link, and send one reminder three days later. Do that on every happy job, and the reviews start stacking up.

## How many Google reviews does a contractor need?

There is no magic number from Google, but there is a real trust threshold. Ten reviews looks thin. Twenty-five looks legit. Fifty starts to look like a business that shows up, finishes jobs, and gets called back.

A company with 50 recent reviews usually feels safer than a competitor with 150 old reviews from years ago. Recency matters. A steady trickle of new reviews tells people the business is active right now.

For local search, the goal is not just volume. The goal is volume, rating, and freshness. A 4.8-star average across 50 reviews beats a perfect score with three reviews and a profile that looks abandoned.

## Ask for Google reviews at the right time

Timing matters more than most owners think. Ask when the customer is happiest, not a week later when the job is a blur.

### Best times to ask

- Right after a successful repair or install.
- At the final walkthrough, before the crew leaves.
- After the invoice is paid and the customer says thanks.
- After a maintenance visit, when the home is back to normal.

A plumber fixing a burst pipe should ask after the water is back on and the customer is relieved. An HVAC tech should ask after the house is cool again. An electrician should ask after the lights are back and the breaker stops tripping.

### What to say

Keep it short and plain. Nobody wants a speech.

- “Glad we got that taken care of. Would you mind leaving a quick Google review?”
- “If the work looked good, a review would help a lot. I can text the link.”
- “Small favor, leave an honest Google review so other homeowners know what to expect.”

That last part matters. Say honest. Customers trust it more, and it lowers the weird pressure some people feel when they hear “five-star review.”

## Make it dead simple to leave a review

Every extra step kills reviews. If a customer has to search your name, find the right profile, log in, and guess where to click, most will bail.

A contractor should always send a direct Google review link. One tap is better than a paragraph of instructions.

### Use these tools together

1. **Text message review link**. This gets the best response rate for most trades.
2. **Email follow-up**. Good backup for office-friendly customers.
3. **QR code on invoices and leave-behinds**. Useful for in-person jobs and repeat clients.
4. **Office follow-up**. Someone in the office can call top customers who already praised the work.

A direct link cuts friction. That is the whole game. A customer in Pittsburgh, especially after a messy roof leak or a broken furnace, is not trying to do homework.

### Build one review flow for every job

The best process is boring:

- Job finishes.
- Tech or office sends the review link.
- Customer gets one reminder if they do not respond.
- Any good review gets a public reply.

Do that long enough and the profile fills in without much drama.

## How to get your first 50 Google reviews faster

The first 10 are the hardest. After that, momentum helps. People trust you more when they can see other people trusted you first.

### Start with your happiest customers

Do not begin with the biggest complainers or the people who wanted the cheapest quote. Start with the customers who already thanked you in person.

Look at:

- Repeat maintenance customers.
- Seasonal tune-up clients.
- Warranty calls you fixed fast.
- Bigger jobs that ended cleanly.
- Referrals from good customers.

A roofing company with a clean reroof job and a happy homeowner should ask the same day. An HVAC company can ask after a tune-up or new install. An electrician can ask after a panel upgrade or a tricky troubleshooting job.

### Use your old customer list

Most trade businesses sit on a pile of past customers and never touch them again. That is lazy money.

Go through the last 6 to 24 months of completed jobs and pull the names of people who had good experiences. Send a personal message, not a blast that sounds like a robot wrote it after three beers.

A simple note works:

- “Hey Mike, glad we could get the furnace back up quickly. If you have 30 seconds, a Google review would really help.”

That kind of message gets replies because it sounds human.

### Create a small review target

Do not say, “We need more reviews.” That is not a plan.

Set a number. For example:

- 5 review requests per day.
- 20 review requests per week.
- 1 reminder after 3 days.
- 1 office follow-up for top customers each week.

At a 20 percent response rate, 100 good asks can produce 20 reviews. At a 40 percent response rate, 100 good asks can produce 40 reviews. That is why the first 50 is not a mystery. It is a numbers game.

## What to do when reviews are slow

Sometimes the customer is happy and still does not leave a review. That is normal. People get distracted, and then the dishwasher starts leaking.

### Fix the process, not the blame

If review counts are flat, look at the process first.

- Are you asking the same day?
- Is the link easy to tap?
- Is the message too long?
- Is someone actually sending the follow-up?
- Are techs forgetting to ask in person?

Most problems are boring process problems. That is good news, because boring problems are easier to fix.

### Don’t chase every customer forever

One follow-up is fine. Two is usually enough. After that, stop.

Pestering people for reviews makes the whole thing feel cheap. A customer who likes your work might still ignore the first text. That does not mean they hate you. It means they are busy.

### Use team incentives carefully

Some contractors pay bonuses for reviews. That can work, but do not turn it into a circus.

Reward the process, not fake praise. Bonus the tech for asking at the right time, not for pressuring customers into five stars. Google and customers both notice when a business gets weird about it.

## How to respond to every Google review

Replying to reviews helps more than most owners think. It shows people the business is alive and paying attention.

### Reply to positive reviews

Keep it short and local.

- Thank the customer.
- Mention the job type.
- Include a service keyword naturally.

Example:

“Thanks for the review, John. Glad the furnace tune-up went smoothly. Call anytime if you need HVAC help.”

That kind of reply helps with trust and local SEO. It also makes the profile look maintained instead of forgotten.

### Reply to negative reviews

Do not argue in public. That is how a one-star review turns into a comedy show.

A better reply looks like this:

- Thank them for the feedback.
- Say you want to make it right.
- Move the conversation offline.

Example:

“Sorry to hear that, Sarah. This is not the experience we want for customers. Please call the office so we can look into it.”

Calm beats defensive every time.

## A 30-day plan to reach your first 50 Google reviews

Here is the simple version.

### Week 1

- Clean up the Google Business Profile.
- Make sure the review link works.
- Write one short text script.
- Print a QR code for invoices and trucks.

### Week 2

- Ask every happy customer.
- Send one follow-up after three days.
- Reply to every new review.
- Have office staff follow up with the best customers.

### Week 3

- Pull the last 12 months of happy customers.
- Send personal texts or emails.
- Ask techs to mention reviews before they leave the job.

### Week 4

- Check which jobs produced reviews.
- Double down on the best ask method.
- Tighten the wording.
- Keep the same routine next month.

A small contractor in Pittsburgh or anywhere else can build momentum fast once the process is repeatable. The goal is not to make one giant push and quit. The goal is to make review requests part of the normal job closeout.

## Final thought

Getting your first 50 Google reviews is not about luck. It is about asking at the right time, making the link easy, and following up without being annoying.

Do that on every good job, and the profile starts to do its job. More reviews lead to more calls, and more calls lead to more reviews. That part actually is a loop worth building.
