## How often should a general contractor post on Google Business Profile?

The short answer is four to five posts a month. That is enough to keep your profile active without turning marketing into another job site headache. If work is slow, one good post a week still beats ten rushed posts in one day. citeturn931298search0

A practical cadence looks like this:

- One post per week for most general contractors.
- Two posts per week during busy season, storm season, or when you are pushing a specific service.
- One post every two weeks only if you are solo, swamped, and still adding fresh photos and reviews.

Google also archives posts older than six months unless a date range is set. That is why a regular posting habit matters. A profile with a fresh update looks alive. A profile with one dusty post from spring looks abandoned, even if the crew is booked solid. citeturn931298search0

## Best Google Business Profile posting schedule for a general contractor

A good schedule does not need to be fancy. It needs to be repeatable.

### One post a week

Use this if you have a normal workload and do not want marketing to eat your lunch. Post one finished project, one before-and-after, or one short tip every week. Over a month, that gives you four fresh signals and four chances to show real work.

### Two posts a week

Use this if you are growing, running ads, or pushing seasonal work. One post can be a project recap. The other can be a promo, a team update, or a service reminder. This cadence is strong for remodelers, roofers, and exterior contractors with active pipelines.

### Once every two weeks

This is the bare minimum. It works if you are tiny and your photos are strong. It is not ideal, but it is better than disappearing for a season and hoping for the best.

For most general contractors, the sweet spot is one post a week, plus extra posts when you have something real to say. That could be a basement finish in Mt. Lebanon, a roof replacement in Ross Township, or a new deck in Cranberry. Local examples matter because people want to see jobs that look like their own house, not stock photos from a brochure factory.

## What to post on Google Business Profile

Google supports updates, offers, and events, and posts can include photos and videos. That gives contractors plenty of room to keep things useful without writing a novel. citeturn931298search0

### Finished project photos

This is the easiest post type. Show the job before, during, and after. Add one sentence about the problem and one sentence about the fix. A kitchen remodel in Squirrel Hill tells a better story than a generic caption about quality craftsmanship.

### Before-and-after posts

These work because people can spot the difference in two seconds. A cracked retaining wall, a sagging porch, or a water-damaged ceiling makes the result obvious. No one needs a paragraph to understand the value.

### Service-specific posts

Use posts to promote the work you actually want. Examples include roof replacements, deck builds, basement finishing, framing, siding, and storm damage repair. If one service makes better margins, post about it more often.

### Seasonal reminders

Spring is for decks, patios, and exterior repairs. Fall is for weatherproofing and prep work. Winter is for interior remodels, insulation, and projects homeowners kept putting off. If the season changes, your posts should change too.

### Reviews and team highlights

A good review screenshot and a real crew photo can do a lot of heavy lifting. People want to know who is showing up at the house. They also want to know the crew can read a tape measure and a room. Both help.

## Common Google Business Profile mistakes contractors make

The biggest mistake is posting too little. The second biggest is posting empty fluff. Nobody needs a caption that says, Another great day on the job site. That tells the customer nothing.

Another common mistake is stuffing a phone number into the post description. Google says phone numbers in post descriptions may get rejected if they cannot be verified as connected to the business. Use the profile's contact options instead of trying to cram everything into the caption. citeturn931298search1

A third mistake is leaving old offers online too long. Google archives posts older than six months unless you set a date range. That means your old spring promo should not still be pretending to be current in November. citeturn931298search0

The last mistake is overthinking it. A clean photo, a short caption, and a real call to action beat a polished post no one ever publishes.

## A simple 30-day posting plan for general contractors

Here is an easy month that works for most contractors.

- Week 1: Post a finished project with one photo and one short win.
- Week 2: Post a before-and-after image with a short explanation of the fix.
- Week 3: Post a service-specific update, like deck builds or basement finishing.
- Week 4: Post a review, team photo, or seasonal reminder.

If you want to add a fifth post, use it for an offer or a timely project update. Google supports recurring posts, so this can be scheduled in advance instead of handled one stressful morning at a time. citeturn931298search0

The point is consistency. A contractor in Pittsburgh does not need to become a content creator. They just need to show proof of work often enough that customers stop wondering if the business is still active.
