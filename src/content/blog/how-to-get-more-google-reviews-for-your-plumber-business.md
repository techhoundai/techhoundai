# How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Plumber Business

Google reviews are not decoration. They are the new version of a neighbor saying, "Call this guy, he fixed my burst pipe." The good news is you do not need a big marketing budget to get them. You need a repeatable ask, a direct review link, and a tech or office person who remembers to send it.

Most plumbing shops can add 5 to 15 new reviews a month when the request happens right after a solid job. In a city like Pittsburgh, that steady pace matters more than a random pile of reviews from three years ago.

## How to get more Google reviews for your plumber business

The short answer is simple. Ask every happy customer, make the link easy, and follow up once.

A plumber with 38 recent reviews often looks more active than one with 140 old ones. Google and customers both like momentum. Fresh reviews also give you more chances to mention the service you actually sell, like drain cleaning, water heater replacement, sump pumps, and leak repair.

The target is not perfection. The target is consistency. If one technician closes four jobs a day, even a 25 percent review rate can create a strong monthly flow.

## The best time to ask for a Google review

Ask after the customer sees the fix and before the paperwork gets buried.

That usually means:
- The leak is stopped.
- The water is back on.
- The customer has had a minute to breathe.
- The invoice is ready.

The ask works best while the relief is fresh. A frozen pipe repair at 8 a.m. feels huge to the homeowner. By dinner, it is just another bill in the pile.

A simple script works better than a fancy speech:

"Glad we got that taken care of. If you were happy with the work, could you leave a Google review? I’ll text you the link."

That is it. Short. Direct. No awkward speech about supporting the small business economy.

For emergency calls, wait until the customer calms down. A flooded basement is not the moment for a review request. Follow up later that day or the next morning.

## How to make review requests automatic

The easiest way to get more reviews is to stop relying on memory.

Use one direct review link for the business. Put it in:
- Text messages
- Email follow-ups
- Invoices
- QR codes on leave-behinds
- A short link on the website

Automation helps because your office staff will forget on busy days. So will techs. Humans are great, but they are not reliable software.

A basic setup can cost $20 to $100 a month if it sits inside a CRM or text platform. QR stickers and invoice cards usually cost under $50 to print. That is cheap compared with paying for ads every time you need a lead.

Good timing beats fancy wording. Send the first request within an hour of the job. Send one follow-up 48 hours later if they have not responded. After that, stop. Nobody likes a pest.

Do not ask for a review in exchange for a discount, gift card, or freebie. That can backfire and it looks fake. Fake reviews smell bad from a mile away.

## What to do when a customer hesitates or leaves a bad review

Not every customer will be thrilled, and that is normal. Some people had a rough day before you arrived. Some think every bill is too high. Some wanted a brand new house for the price of a repair.

If a customer hesitates, do not push. Ask for honest feedback instead. Sometimes they only need a reminder that the review helps the next homeowner pick a plumber with confidence.

If you get a bad review, answer fast. Same day is best. Keep it calm, short, and human.

A good response sounds like this:
"Sorry we missed the mark here. Please call the office so we can look at what happened and make it right."

That works better than arguing in public. A comment fight on Google makes the business look busy and defensive. Nobody wants to hire the plumber who types like he is suing the customer.

If the review is fake or clearly spam, flag it. But do not waste all morning staring at one angry sentence. Real life is out there. Toilets are still overflowing.

## How Google Business Profile and reviews work together

Reviews help most when the rest of your Google Business Profile is in decent shape.

That means:
- The business category is correct.
- The service area is filled out.
- The phone number is right.
- The website works on a phone.
- Recent job photos are posted.
- The profile has steady activity.

Reviews are only one signal. If your profile looks abandoned, more reviews will not fix everything. It is like polishing a truck with a flat tire.

A steady review flow is also better than one giant burst. Three new reviews a week for a few months looks natural. Twenty reviews in two days can look weird unless you just opened or rebranded.

In Pittsburgh, this matters for plumbers serving places like the South Hills, Cranberry, Dormont, and Lawrenceville. People search fast, compare fast, and call the business that looks active and local.

## A 30-day review plan for plumbers

Here is a simple plan that works without turning the office into a call center.

### Week 1
Set up the review link, the text template, and the follow-up message. Give every tech one script to use.

### Week 2
Add the link to invoices, thank-you texts, and email follow-ups. Put a QR code on a leave-behind card.

### Week 3
Start asking after every completed job. Focus on customers who were clearly happy.

### Week 4
Reply to every new review. Thank the good ones. Handle the rough ones without drama.

If you do this for 90 days, the results usually show up. The shop starts looking established. That helps with trust, click-through rate, and call volume.

## Final thoughts

More Google reviews do not come from being more charming. They come from having a system and sticking to it.

For plumbers, the best system is boring in the best way. Finish the job, ask at the right time, send the link by text, follow up once, and keep your Google Business Profile active. That beats hoping customers remember to find you later.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a plumber need?
There is no magic number. A new plumbing shop should aim for 20 to 30 to stop looking brand new. Fifty or more makes the business look established in most local markets.

### Should plumbers ask for reviews by text or email?
Text usually gets better results. People open texts faster, and the review link is easier to tap on a phone. Email still helps as a backup.

### How often should a plumber post on Google Business Profile?
Once a week is a solid target. A photo, a short update, or a job recap keeps the profile active without wasting time.

### What should a plumber do after a bad Google review?
Respond quickly, stay calm, and offer to fix the issue offline. Never argue point by point in the review response. That just gives the internet more drama.

### Can you ask for Google reviews after emergency plumbing calls?
Yes, but wait until the customer has calmed down. A flooded basement or no-hot-water call is stressful. Send the request later the same day or the next morning.
