# How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Roofer Business

Google reviews are one of the fastest ways for a roofing company to look trustworthy before anyone calls. The trick is not begging harder. It is asking at the right moment, making the link easy, and following up once.

A roofer with 4.8 stars and 32 recent reviews usually looks stronger than a company with a perfect rating and six reviews from 2022. In Pittsburgh, that difference can decide who gets the call after a storm, a leak, or a missing shingle.

## How many Google reviews does a roofer need?

A new roofing company should aim for 20 to 30 Google reviews just to stop looking brand new. Once you pass 50, the profile starts to look established. Past 100, you are usually in good shape unless the competitors are aggressive.

The number matters, but recency matters more. Ten fresh reviews from the last three months can beat 40 stale reviews from years ago. That is especially true in roofing, where homeowners want proof that real jobs are happening now.

A good target is simple:

- **0 to 10 reviews:** looks new
- **20 to 30 reviews:** looks real
- **50 reviews or more:** looks established
- **100 reviews or more:** hard to ignore

If you close five roofing jobs a week and ask every customer, getting one or two reviews a week is realistic. That adds up fast.

## Ask for the review at the right time

Timing matters more than most roofers think. The best moment is right after the job is done and the customer is happy. That could be after a full roof replacement, a repair that stopped a leak, or the final walkthrough.

Do not wait two weeks and hope they remember your name. By then, they are back to work, the dog ate the invoice, and the memory is gone.

The best time to ask is when the customer says something like, “Looks great,” or “Thanks for getting here so fast.” That is your opening. The job is fresh, the relief is real, and the odds are better.

A simple script works fine:

> Glad we got that taken care of. If you’ve got 30 seconds, a Google review would help a lot. Here’s the link.

For storm damage jobs, ask after the insurance work is wrapped up and the customer signs off. That is often the moment they are most relieved and most likely to help.

### Best timing by job type

- **Emergency leak repair:** ask the same day
- **Roof replacement:** ask at the final walkthrough
- **Insurance claim work:** ask after the final approval
- **Small repairs:** ask when the customer confirms the fix held up

## Make leaving a Google review easy

A customer will not hunt for your profile unless you make them. Your job is to remove every extra click.

Text the direct review link. Put a QR code on your invoice, estimate sheet, truck handout, or thank-you card. If someone has to search for your business name again, you have already lost half the reviews.

A printed stack of 100 review cards usually costs about $25 to $60. QR stickers are cheap too. That is a lot less than buying leads you should have earned for free.

Use all three of these:

1. **Text message:** best for speed
2. **Email:** good backup for customers who read email
3. **Printed QR code:** useful in person and in the office

The link should go straight to the review form. Do not send people to the homepage and make them dig around. That is how reviews die quietly.

## What roofers should never do with reviews

Do not offer a discount, gift card, or “thank you bonus” for a review. That gets messy fast and can make the review look fake. It is also a bad habit if you care about long-term trust.

Do not gate reviews either. That means asking happy customers to leave public reviews and sending unhappy customers somewhere private instead. Google does not love that, and customers can smell it.

Do not buy fake reviews. A few extra stars are not worth getting your profile trashed later. One bad cleanup job from a bad reviewer can cause more pain than the fake review helped.

Do not ask five different people from the same roofing crew to bug the customer. One clean request is enough. A customer does not want to feel like they are being hunted by the whole truck fleet.

## A simple review system for a roofing company

You do not need a fancy CRM project to do this right. You need a repeatable process that happens after every job.

### Step 1: tag every happy customer

At job closeout, mark the customer as review-ready. That can be in your CRM, a spreadsheet, or even a shared task list if that is what you have.

### Step 2: send the text within 24 hours

The shorter the gap, the better. If the job ended today, send the review request today. If it ended late Friday, send it Saturday morning or Monday morning.

### Step 3: follow up once

If they do not respond, send one reminder three to five days later. Do not turn it into a six-message campaign. People notice when you get pushy.

### Step 4: reply to every review

Reply to good reviews and bad ones. Keep it short, personal, and specific. Mention the project type when it makes sense, like a roof replacement in Brentwood or a leak repair in the North Hills.

That helps with trust and keeps the profile active. It also shows the company is alive, which sounds basic because it is.

## How Google reviews help roofing leads

Reviews do more than make the profile look nice. They help customers choose you faster. They also give Google more confidence that your business is active, trusted, and real.

A roofing company with steady reviews tends to win more clicks from the map results. More clicks usually means more calls. That is the whole game.

Reviews also help when someone is comparing you with two other roofers. People do not read every detail. They skim stars, review count, recent photos, and whether the company looks responsive.

In a market like Pittsburgh, that matters. Homeowners are busy, weather changes fast, and nobody wants to interview five roofers before lunch.

## A 30-day plan to get more reviews

Start with the last 20 to 30 happy customers. Ask the ones who already liked the work, paid on time, and sent a thank-you text.

Here is a simple plan:

- **Week 1:** build your direct review link and QR code
- **Week 2:** text ten recent customers
- **Week 3:** ask every completed job for a review
- **Week 4:** reply to every review and keep the habit going

If you have a decent close rate, five to ten new reviews in 30 days is realistic. That is enough to move a profile from sleepy to credible.

The big mistake is treating reviews like a one-time project. They work best when they become part of the closeout process, right after the roof is finished and the customer is still relieved.

## Final thought

Roofers do not need clever marketing copy to win more reviews. They need a clean process, a direct link, and a habit of asking when the job is done.
