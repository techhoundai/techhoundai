# How to Market Your Roofing Business in the Off-Season

The best off-season marketing for a roofing business is simple: stay visible, stay local, and keep sending reminders while everyone else goes quiet. That means Google Business Profile posts, review requests, email to past customers, and service pages that still rank when the weather turns cold.

If a roofing company only markets when shingles are flying off the roof in July, winter gets slow fast. The phone does not care that you were busy in summer. Customers forget fast, and the business that keeps showing up in their feed, inbox, and search results usually gets the call.

## What off-season marketing should do for a roofing business

Off-season marketing has one job, keep your name in front of people until they need a roof again. It should also help you get found for smaller jobs, like leak repairs, inspections, flashing fixes, and storm damage calls.

A good off-season plan does not need a giant budget. It needs consistency. In Pittsburgh, that matters because the weather swings from mild to miserable before lunch, and roof leaks do not wait for spring.

### Focus on the jobs people still need in winter

A lot of roofers market like every lead is a full replacement. That is a mistake. Most off-season searches are for repairs, inspections, emergency tarping, and insurance help.

Those are the jobs to talk about first. They keep your calendar moving, and they are easier to sell than a full tear-off when homeowners are already stressed.

## Keep your Google Business Profile active

Your Google Business Profile should not go stale just because the season changed. Post once a week if you can, or at least twice a month. That keeps fresh activity on the profile and gives people a reason to click.

Use real job photos, short captions, and a clear service mention. A post about a winter leak repair in Mt. Lebanon beats a generic graphic with stock shingles and blue sky nonsense.

### What roofers should post each week

Use simple post ideas that take 10 minutes, not 2 hours.

- Before and after roof repairs
- Storm damage inspection photos
- Ice dam prevention tips
- Flashing repairs around chimneys and vents
- Gutter and edge detail photos
- Short updates about emergency roof service

The goal is not poetry. The goal is proof. People want to see that the company is active, local, and doing real work.

### Reply to every review

Review replies matter because they add fresh text to the profile and show that the business is alive. Keep them short and natural.

For example, thank the customer, mention the service, and include the area when it fits. A simple reply like, “Appreciate the review. Glad we could help with the leak repair in Cranberry Township,” does the job.

## Send past customers a simple email campaign

Most roofing businesses sit on a list of past customers and never touch it. That list is money sitting in a drawer.

A winter email campaign can bring back old customers who need maintenance, repairs, or a second opinion. It also keeps referrals warm. People may not need a roof this month, but they remember the roofer who stayed in touch.

### Use a short three-email sequence

A simple sequence works better than a long newsletter nobody reads.

1. **Email 1, late fall:** remind customers to check for loose shingles, flashing issues, and clogged gutters.
2. **Email 2, mid-winter:** talk about leak signs, ice dams, and attic moisture.
3. **Email 3, early spring:** push inspections after freeze-thaw cycles and storm damage.

Keep each email short. One problem, one tip, one call to action. That is enough.

### Segment by customer type when possible

Not every customer should get the same message. A homeowner with a 3-year-old roof does not need the same note as someone with a 20-year-old roof.

If the list is simple, that is fine. Even a basic campaign to all past customers is better than doing nothing. If the software can tag jobs by roof age, roof type, or service area, even better.

## Build service pages that still rank in winter

A roofing website should not only talk about replacements. The off-season is a good time to build pages for the jobs people search for when the weather gets rough.

Strong pages can rank for terms like roof leak repair, emergency roof tarp service, roof inspection, storm damage roof repair, and chimney flashing repair. Those pages give Google more reasons to send the right traffic.

### Add pages for high-intent winter services

Start with the services that lead to calls, not vanity content.

- Roof leak repair
- Emergency tarping
- Roof inspections
- Storm damage repair
- Flashing repair
- Ice dam damage help
- Gutter-related roof edge issues

Each page should explain the problem, the fix, and the area served. If a company works around Pittsburgh, South Hills, Cranberry, and Monroeville, say so clearly.

### Use service area pages the right way

A lot of contractors create city pages that say almost nothing. That does not help anyone.

A better page mentions the services offered in that town, common roof problems in that area, and a few local details. Keep it honest. If the company rarely works in a town, do not pretend it is the second headquarters.

## Use reviews and job photos as proof

Off-season marketing works better when the business can prove it has done the work. Roofers have a big advantage here, because every job produces photos.

A few good before-and-after shots can do more than a fancy slogan ever will. Post them on the website, Google Business Profile, Facebook, and your email campaigns.

### Ask for reviews right after the job wraps up

The best time to ask for a review is right after the customer sees the finished work. The roof looks good, the leak is fixed, and the stress is gone.

That is when people are most likely to leave a positive review. Send the link by text, make it easy, and do not make the customer hunt for it like a lost wrench.

### Show the work, not just the truck

Customers care less about a branded truck photo than they do about a clean repair, tight flashing, or a solid cleanup. Show the actual job.

A gallery of real work gives your profile and website a steady stream of proof. It also helps the business stand out from the roofer down the street who still thinks a logo and a phone number count as marketing.

## What to spend and what to skip in the off-season

You do not need to dump money into broad ads just to stay busy. A lot of off-season lead flow comes from low-cost work that compounds over time.

A practical monthly budget might look like this:

- $0 to $200 for DIY content and email tools
- $100 to $500 for review systems, scheduling tools, or automation
- $300 to $1,500 if you want help with website updates, SEO, and follow-up systems

That is enough for most local roofing companies to stay active without lighting cash on fire.

### Skip the shiny stuff that does not book jobs

Do not spend the whole winter on brand videos, random giveaways, or social posts with no call to action. That stuff feels busy, but it usually does not move the calendar.

Focus on the boring things that work: search visibility, proof, email follow-up, and clear service pages. Boring wins. Boring also pays the bills.

## A simple off-season plan for roofers

If the goal is more calls, keep the plan simple.

Post once a week on Google Business Profile. Send one useful email each month. Ask for reviews after every job. Add or improve one service page each month. That is enough to build momentum.

Do that for three or four months, and the business stops relying on the busy season to carry the whole year.

## FAQ

### How do roofers get leads in the off-season?

Roofers get off-season leads by staying visible in Google search, keeping their Google Business Profile active, and sending reminders to past customers. The best leads usually come from repair, inspection, and storm damage searches, not just full replacements.

### How often should a roofer post on Google Business Profile?

Once a week is a strong pace for most roofing businesses. If that is too much, post twice a month and stay consistent. Fresh photos and short service updates work better than long, polished posts nobody reads.

### Should a roofing company send email campaigns in winter?

Yes. Winter is a good time to remind past customers about leaks, ice dams, and inspection needs. A short email every few weeks keeps the business in front of people without annoying them.

### What winter services should roofers promote?

Roof leak repair, emergency tarping, storm damage repair, roof inspections, and flashing repair are the big ones. These services match the problems homeowners notice most when temperatures drop.

### How much should a roofing business spend on off-season marketing?

Many roofing companies can make progress with a few hundred dollars a month. If the website, review process, and email setup need work, spending a little more on automation and SEO usually makes more sense than spending it all on ads.
