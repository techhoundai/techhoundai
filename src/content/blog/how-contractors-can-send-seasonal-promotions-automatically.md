# How Contractors Can Send Seasonal Promotions Automatically

Seasonal promotions automatically are the easiest way to keep past customers buying when the weather changes or work slows down. The setup is simple: one clean customer list, a few tags, and scheduled emails or texts sent before the season hits.

A good campaign does not feel like spam. It feels like a reminder from a shop that remembers the customer, knows the season, and has a real job to sell.

## What seasonal promotions should contractors send?

The best seasonal promotion is the next logical job. Do not send random discounts because you saw them in a retail ad. A homeowner with a broken furnace does not care about 15 percent off deck staining.

### Good offers by trade

- HVAC: furnace tune-up, AC checkup, filter replacement, thermostat upgrade
- Plumbing: drain cleaning, water heater flush, frozen pipe prep, sump pump test
- Roofing: storm inspection, leak check, gutter cleaning, attic ventilation check
- Landscaping: spring cleanup, mulch refresh, fall aeration, leaf cleanup
- Electricians: panel inspection, generator service, outdoor lighting check
- Painters and carpenters: interior refresh, trim repair, deck repair, winter booking for spring jobs

The point is to sell work that fits the weather and the calendar. A Pittsburgh HVAC shop should not wait for the first freezing night. By then, every competitor is already texting.

## Build the customer list before you automate anything

Automation only works if the list is clean. A pile of old phone numbers and half-filled emails is not a list. It is junk with a spreadsheet label.

### Collect contact info at three moments

1. Estimate.
2. Job completion.
3. Invoice or payment.

Ask for both email and mobile number. Email is better for photos, longer explanations, and offers. Text is better for reminders and quick last-call messages.

### Tag customers by job and season

Use simple tags like these:

- HVAC maintenance
- furnace customer
- AC customer
- plumbing repair
- drain cleaning
- roof repair
- storm damage
- spring cleanup
- fall cleanup

Add a last-service date too. That one field matters more than people think. A customer who had a furnace tune-up last month should not get another one this week.

### Segment by geography when it helps

If the business serves multiple towns, split the list by area. A shop in Pittsburgh can send one message to the North Hills and another to the South Hills if the offers are different. That keeps messages relevant and stops the “this does not apply to me” problem.

A relevant message gets read. A generic blast gets deleted.

## How to send seasonal promotions automatically

The setup does not need fancy software. It needs a repeatable flow that somebody on the team can understand.

### Step 1: Pick one system of record

Use a CRM or service platform that already stores customer history. Jobber, FieldPulse, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or a basic email tool can all work if the data lives in one place.

Do not split contacts across three tools unless you enjoy cleanup work.

### Step 2: Build one trigger

A trigger is the event that starts the campaign. Common triggers are:

- date-based, like every September 15
- tag-based, like everyone marked “furnace customer”
- inactivity-based, like customers who have not booked in 12 months

For seasonal work, date-based triggers are the easiest. They keep the campaign on schedule even when the office gets busy.

### Step 3: Write a short message with one offer

Keep the message simple. One service. One deadline. One call to action.

A good promo says:

- what the service is
- why now matters
- how to book
- what the customer gets

A bad promo says a lot and sells nothing.

### Step 4: Add a follow-up sequence

One message is not enough. Use a short sequence instead:

- Email 1, announcement
- Email or text 5 to 7 days later, reminder
- Final reminder 3 to 5 days before the deadline

That is usually enough. More than that starts to feel needy.

### Step 5: Track replies and bookings

The best automation sends work to the office, not into the void. Track opens, clicks, replies, and booked jobs. If a campaign gets attention but no jobs, the offer is weak. If nobody opens it, the subject line or timing is bad.

Most email tools cost around $20 to $100 per month. Texting usually adds per-message fees. That is cheap compared with losing even one furnace replacement or a full week of spring cleanup calls.

## When should contractors send seasonal promotions?

Timing matters more than design. The best offer in the world dies if it lands after the season has already turned.

### Use the 4 to 6 week rule

Send the first message 4 to 6 weeks before the season starts. That gives customers time to think, compare, and book.

For example:

- HVAC heating offers: late August to mid-September
- HVAC cooling offers: late March to mid-April
- Plumbing freeze-prep offers: before the first real cold snap
- Roofing inspections: before storm season, not after
- Landscaping: late winter for spring cleanup, late summer for fall aeration

### Match timing to local weather

Local weather should drive the schedule. In Pittsburgh, the first 40-degree morning can show up fast. If the furnace tune-up email goes out after that, the shop is late. Same story with summer AC work. Nobody wants to think about it when the attic feels like a pizza oven.

### Use urgency without sounding fake

Do not write “last chance” every week. People can smell fake urgency from a mile away. Real urgency sounds like this: “We are booking fall furnace tune-ups now, and the schedule is filling fast.”

That is honest. Honesty books jobs.

## What to put in the message

The offer matters, but the wording matters too. A lot of contractor marketing fails because the message sounds like a flyer nobody asked for.

### Keep the message short

Short messages get read. Long messages get skimmed on the truck, then forgotten.

A strong seasonal message usually has:

- a subject line
- one sentence about why the offer matters
- one clear action
- one phone number or booking link

### Use proof, not hype

If possible, include one of these:

- a before-and-after photo
- a short customer quote
- a local neighborhood reference
- a simple list of what the service includes

For example, an HVAC shop can say the tune-up includes a filter check, safety inspection, and thermostat test. That is better than “top-tier service at unbeatable value.” Nobody believes that stuff anyway.

### Offer the right incentive

Not every promotion needs a discount. Sometimes the better offer is convenience.

Examples:

- priority booking
- free inspection with service
- seasonal safety check
- bundled add-on
- fixed-price maintenance visit

Discounts can help, but they should not be the whole plan. Cheap work attracts cheap customers. That is not a business model. It is a headache.

## Simple campaign examples that work

Here are a few examples a trade business can copy without hiring a marketing committee.

### HVAC fall campaign

- Email 1: “Book your furnace tune-up before the cold hits.”
- Email 2: “We are filling October slots now.”
- Text reminder: “Still want a furnace tune-up? Reply YES and we will send times.”

A list of 300 past HVAC customers only needs a 2 percent booking rate to produce 6 jobs. If those jobs average $225, that is $1,350 from one clean campaign.

### Plumbing winter campaign

- Email 1: frozen pipe prep and water heater check
- Email 2: drain cleaning or sump pump reminder
- Text reminder: last open slots before the hard freeze

This works because it solves a real problem before it becomes a flooded basement.

### Landscaping spring campaign

- Email 1: spring cleanup and mulch
- Email 2: aeration and first mow schedule
- Text reminder: “Spring routes are nearly full.”

Landscaping is perfect for automation because the work returns every year. The list already knows the business. It just needs a reminder.

## What to avoid

A few mistakes keep showing up in contractor campaigns.

- blasting the whole list with the same offer
- sending messages with no booking link or phone number
- waiting until the season is over
- sending too many reminders
- using different tools that do not talk to each other
- forgetting to remove customers who already booked

Automation should reduce office chaos, not create a new kind of mess.

## Final thought

The contractors who win seasonal work are usually not the ones with the flashiest ads. They are the ones who send the right message to the right people before everyone else wakes up.

A good system is simple: collect contacts, tag them, schedule the campaign, and track bookings. Do that well, and seasonal marketing stops being a scramble.
