# How to Set Service Areas on Google Business Profile for Contractors in 2026

Google Business Profile service areas tell customers where you actually work. For contractors, the right list is simple: add the cities, ZIP codes, and neighborhoods you really serve, then keep that same footprint on your website and service pages. Set them badly and you confuse Google, confuse customers, and end up driving too far for junk leads.

The good news is that this is not hard. Google lets you set a service area based on cities, ZIP codes, or other places, and it tells business owners to be specific and accurate. That is the whole trick.

## What Google Business Profile service areas actually do

A service area helps Google understand where your business goes to work. That matters for plumbers, electricians, HVAC techs, roofers, painters, and general contractors who travel to the customer instead of waiting for walk-ins.

If you do not meet customers at your business address, Google also lets you hide your address. If customers do visit your shop, office, or yard, keep the address visible and set the service area too. Do not try to play both sides.

A service area is not a magic ranking button. It is a location signal. It works best when your reviews, categories, website, and job photos all point to the same towns.

## How to set service areas on Google Business Profile

Start with the places you already get paid to work in. Pull up your last 20 jobs and look for the repeat towns and ZIP codes. Those are your real service areas, not the ones you wish sounded bigger.

A good starting list is usually 5 to 10 areas for a small contractor, 8 to 15 for a growing crew, and 15 to 20 for a bigger company with more trucks. More is not better if half the list is stretch territory.

Use city names or ZIP codes that people actually search. For example, a Pittsburgh plumber might list Pittsburgh, Dormont, Mt. Lebanon, Bethel Park, and Scott Township. An HVAC company might add a few nearby suburbs, but only if the techs really go there on a normal week.

Keep the list tight enough that a homeowner would not roll their eyes. If you would not gladly send a truck there on a Tuesday afternoon, leave it off the profile.

## What service areas help with, and what they do not

Service areas help with relevance and customer clarity. They tell Google and the customer, "Yes, this business comes to you here."

They do not fix a weak profile by themselves. If your category is wrong, your reviews are thin, your website is slow, or your contact info is messy, the service area list will not save you.

Think of it like labeling your tool trailer. A neat label helps people find the right wrench. It does not make the wrench sharper.

The best results come when your GBP service areas match your website service pages, your review locations, and the towns mentioned in your photo captions and blog posts. That consistency helps Google trust the pattern.

## Common contractor mistakes with service areas

The biggest mistake is stuffing in every town within 50 miles. That looks sloppy, and it usually comes from someone trying to rank in places they barely serve.

Another mistake is using a giant region as a shortcut, like "Greater Pittsburgh Area," and leaving it there forever. That is vague. Google prefers actual places, not marketing fog.

A third mistake is creating duplicate profiles for each town. One business should have one profile. Multiple fake profiles can create more problems than leads.

Also, do not hide your address if customers come to your shop. If you do on-site consultations, pickups, or showroom visits, the address matters. Hiding it when customers expect to find you can cost trust fast.

## Best service area setup by trade

Different trades can use different service area shapes.

### Plumbers

Plumbers usually do best with a tighter list. Emergency jobs are time-sensitive, so a smaller radius is usually smarter. In Pittsburgh, that might mean the city and nearby South Hills or North Hills towns.

### Electricians

Electricians can often work a little wider, especially for planned jobs. Still, the list should reflect real dispatch patterns, not every suburb with a nice ring to it.

### HVAC contractors

HVAC companies can usually cover a broader area because installs and maintenance routes are easier to plan. A bigger truck fleet can support more towns, but every town should still be active.

### Roofers and painters

Roofers and painters can go farther when the job size is worth it. Even then, keep the service list tied to where you actually quote and win jobs. Big map area, honest list.

## Quick checklist before you save the profile

Before you hit save, check these five things:

- Your service areas match real jobs from the last few months.
- You used city names or ZIP codes, not vague marketing language.
- Your website says the same towns.
- Your address is visible only if customers actually visit your location.
- Your category, reviews, and photos all support the same service footprint.

If you want one rule to remember, use this one: accurate beats impressive. A clean list of 8 real service areas will beat 40 fake ones every time.

## The simple takeaway

Set your Google Business Profile service areas to the places you actually serve, keep the list tight, and match it to your website. That gives Google a clear signal and gives customers a clear answer.
