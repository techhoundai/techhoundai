Jobber vs FieldPulse vs ServiceTitan is the comparison most contractors should make before buying field service software. The wrong CRM turns into a fancy expense with a login screen. The right one saves time, keeps leads from slipping, and gives the office fewer fires to put out.

This guide breaks down price, feature fit, and team size so you can pick the best CRM for your trade business without sitting through three demos and a pep talk.

## Quick answer: which CRM should you pick?

For most small contractor businesses, Jobber is the easiest pick. It has public pricing, a short learning curve, and enough scheduling, quoting, invoicing, and follow-up tools for a lot of plumbing, HVAC, electrical, and landscaping shops.

FieldPulse is the better middle ground if you want more control over workflows, forms, pricebooks, and communication. ServiceTitan is the heavyweight option. It is built for bigger, busier operations that need deeper dispatching, reporting, and pricebook control.

Here is the short version:

| CRM | Best for | Biggest strength | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jobber | Small to midsize crews | Easy setup and clear pricing | Add-ons can stack up |
| FieldPulse | Teams that want flexible workflows | Strong field operations tools | No public pricing |
| ServiceTitan | Larger, more complex shops | Deep operations and reporting | High cost and heavier setup |

If you run a two-truck shop in Pittsburgh, Jobber or FieldPulse is usually the smarter conversation. If you run a large HVAC operation with dispatchers, comfort advisors, and a real office team, ServiceTitan starts to make sense.

## Jobber vs FieldPulse vs ServiceTitan pricing

Price matters because software is never just the sticker price. It is the sticker price plus setup time, add-ons, user seats, onboarding, and the weird little feature you suddenly need after the sale.

Jobber is the only one of the three with easy-to-find public pricing. Its pricing page shows a Core plan starting at $29 per month, with higher plans scaling up to $499 per month depending on team size and billing. Jobber also sells add-ons like Marketing Suite for $99 per month, Receptionist for $29 per month, and Pipeline for $49 per month.

That is the good part. The catch is simple. A cheap starting plan can become a much bigger monthly bill once you add the pieces that actually matter.

FieldPulse takes a different route. Its pricing page says the platform is seat-based and built around a custom quote. That is fine if you want a tailored setup, but it means you cannot compare the monthly cost in five seconds on a sales page.

ServiceTitan is even more enterprise-style. Its pricing page says it uses per-technician pricing and asks you to request pricing. The package pages show Starter, Essentials, and The Works, but the actual number depends on your shop.

That pricing gap tells you a lot. Jobber sells convenience. FieldPulse sells flexibility. ServiceTitan sells depth.

## Jobber vs FieldPulse vs ServiceTitan features that matter on job sites

A CRM only matters if your crew uses it when things get messy. Pretty dashboards do not book jobs. Good tools do.

### Jobber features

Jobber covers the basics well. It supports booking and scheduling, quotes, invoices, online payments, reporting, client reminders, automated invoice follow-ups, a website, a marketing suite, and a mobile app. It also has integrations for QuickBooks Online, Xero, Zapier, and other common tools.

That makes Jobber a strong pick for a small contractor business that wants to look organized without turning the office into a software department. It is simple enough for a part-time office person to learn fast.

### FieldPulse features

FieldPulse leans harder into field service operations. Its public pricing page and feature pages show scheduling and dispatching, work orders, job management, estimates and invoices, custom workflows, inventory, asset management, customer communication, customer portal, booking portal, maintenance agreements, custom forms, pricebook, fleet tracking, and a built-in phone system.

That matters when your business has more moving parts. If you need tighter control over how leads, jobs, and customer communication move through the day, FieldPulse gives you more knobs to turn.

### ServiceTitan features

ServiceTitan is built for bigger shops that need stronger dispatching, call booking, invoicing, pricebook control, mobile estimates, reporting, payroll management, commission tracking, and customizable memberships. Its product pages also highlight call recording, automated checklists, marketing reporting, GPS tracking, and other tools that help larger teams keep their process tight.

That is great if you actually have the volume to use it. It is not great if your business is still figuring out who handles the phones after lunch.

## Which CRM fits your crew size?

The best CRM is the one that matches how your business works today, not the version of your company you hope to become in five years.

### 1 to 5 people

Pick Jobber unless you already know you need more complexity. Small crews need speed. They need quotes, invoices, reminders, and a clean way to keep leads moving.

A small plumber, roofer, or electrician does not need a monster system. They need fewer missed calls and fewer sticky notes on the dash.

### 5 to 25 people

FieldPulse usually fits here. This is the range where your workflow starts to matter more. You may have office staff, field techs, and a few recurring customers who expect a more organized process.

If your team already argues about who updated the schedule, more flexible workflows will help.

### 25+ people

ServiceTitan starts looking more reasonable here. Bigger teams need better visibility into dispatch, performance, call handling, and reporting. You also start caring more about consistency across techs and crews.

At that point, the software bill should be compared against the cost of sloppy operations. That is a real number, not a spreadsheet fantasy.

## Common mistakes contractors make when choosing a CRM

The first mistake is buying for future size instead of current pain. A five-person company does not need enterprise software just because the demo looked impressive.

The second mistake is choosing based on price alone. Cheap software that nobody uses is expensive. It still costs money, and it wastes time.

The third mistake is ignoring onboarding. Moving contacts, estimates, photos, and job history is where a lot of teams get stuck. If the setup drags on for months, the crew goes back to texts and notebooks.

The fourth mistake is buying features the office will never touch. If you do not need complex pricebooks, do not pay for them. If you do not need advanced reporting, do not pretend you will build a dashboard every Friday morning.

The fifth mistake is not measuring the win. Track a few numbers before and after the switch: lead response time, booked-job rate, invoice turnaround, and how many calls fall through the cracks. If those numbers do not improve, the software is just a tax with better branding.

## Final take on Jobber vs FieldPulse vs ServiceTitan

Jobber is the best starting point for most small trade businesses. It is easier to buy, easier to learn, and easier to live with.

FieldPulse is the strongest middle option for teams that want more control and better field operations tools.

ServiceTitan is the heavyweight. It is expensive, but it earns that cost only when your business is big enough to need the extra horsepower.
