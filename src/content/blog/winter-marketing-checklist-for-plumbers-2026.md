# Winter Marketing Checklist for Plumbers in 2026

Winter is not dead season for plumbers. It is freeze season, water heater season, and the time when slow follow-up gets expensive. This winter marketing checklist for plumbers in 2026 shows the moves that keep the phone ringing when temperatures drop.

The short version is simple: keep your Google Business Profile current, make your website easy to call from, ask for reviews after every job, and send seasonal reminders to past customers. In Pittsburgh, one cold snap can turn a quiet week into a pipe repair sprint.

## Winter marketing checklist for plumbers: update Google Business Profile first

Your Google Business Profile is usually the first place a cold-weather customer checks. If your hours, services, or photos are stale, people assume the business is too.

### Add winter services to your profile

List the jobs people actually search for in winter. That means frozen pipe repair, leak detection, water heater replacement, drain cleaning, sewer line repair, and emergency plumbing service.

Do not bury this stuff under a generic “plumbing services” label. Google and customers both like specific answers.

### Post fresh photos every week

Winter photos do not need to be fancy. A quick shot of a thawed pipe, a water heater install, or a clean truck in front of a job site is enough.

Aim for 2 to 3 new photos a week during peak season. A profile that looks active tends to get more clicks than one that looks abandoned.

### Check holiday hours and service areas

Update holiday hours before Thanksgiving and again before Christmas break. If the shop is closed on certain days, say so clearly.

Also make sure your service area still matches the jobs you want. If you serve Pittsburgh, the North Hills, and surrounding suburbs, say that plainly.

## Fix your website for winter plumbing leads

A lot of plumbing websites look fine until the phone starts ringing. Then the cracks show. Slow pages, buried phone numbers, and generic service pages cost real jobs.

### Build a page for your winter money services

A plumber does not need one giant page that talks about everything. It needs focused pages for the services people search in winter.

Good examples include:

- Frozen pipe repair
- Water heater repair and replacement
- Emergency plumbing service
- Drain cleaning
- Leak detection
- Sump pump repair

Each page should answer three things fast: what the problem is, how fast you can help, and how to call.

### Put the phone number at the top of every page

Winter customers do not want to hunt for contact info. Put the phone number in the header, make it clickable on mobile, and repeat it near the bottom of the page.

The same goes for your quote form. Keep it short. Name, phone number, address, and a short message are enough.

### Add a winter hero message

Your homepage should say something useful in plain English. Try something like: frozen pipe repair, water heater service, and emergency plumbing in Pittsburgh.

That sentence helps both search engines and people who are scanning the page on a cracked phone screen in a cold driveway.

## Ask for Google reviews while the job is fresh

Winter is a great time to ask for reviews because the customer usually feels the pain of the problem right away. They remember the fast response and the clean finish.

### Ask within 24 hours

Do not wait a week. Ask for the review while the job is still fresh in the customer’s mind. A short text after the invoice usually works best.

A simple message is enough: “Thanks for choosing us for the pipe repair today. If you were happy with the work, a Google review would mean a lot.”

### Make review requests part of the closeout process

The easiest system is the one your team actually uses. Put the review ask in the same checklist as payment, photos, and follow-up notes.

If every tech asks the same way, the review count grows faster. That matters when someone compares your shop to the guy down the street with three reviews and a broken website.

### Reply to every review

A reply does two things. It shows customers you pay attention, and it gives Google more activity to read.

Keep the replies short and specific. Mention the service, the neighborhood, or the problem solved when it makes sense.

## Send winter reminders to past customers automatically

Most plumbing businesses already have a list of old customers sitting in a CRM, spreadsheet, or email tool. That list is money if you use it right.

### Send service reminders before the cold hits

Reach out before the first hard freeze. Good winter reminders include water heater flushes, sump pump checks, drain cleaning, and leak inspections.

You are not begging for work. You are reminding people about problems they would rather avoid.

### Segment the list by past job type

Do not send the same message to everyone. A homeowner who had a water heater installed last year should get a different message than someone who called for a clogged main line.

Simple segments work fine:

- Water heater customers
- Drain cleaning customers
- Sewer line customers
- Emergency repair customers

That makes the message feel useful instead of spammy.

### Use text and email together

Email is good for longer reminders. Text is better for fast responses. A short winter promo sent by both usually gets more attention than either channel alone.

A lot of shops ignore this because it feels like office work. That is exactly why it works. Most competitors never get around to it.

## Track what actually brings in winter calls

Winter marketing gets messy fast if nobody tracks results. A few simple numbers will tell you what is working.

### Watch calls from Google Business Profile

Look at calls, website clicks, and direction requests from your profile. If those numbers rise after you post photos or reviews, keep doing it.

### Track calls from the website separately

Use a call tracking number if you can. That tells you whether the homepage, service pages, or winter landing pages are doing the heavy lifting.

### Measure response time

Speed matters more in winter than almost any other season. If your office takes two hours to call back, the customer already tried the next plumber.

A good goal is a response within 10 minutes during business hours and a fast missed-call text after hours.

## What a strong winter plumbing marketing plan looks like

A solid winter plan does not need a huge budget. It needs consistency.

A good monthly rhythm looks like this:

- 2 Google Business Profile posts per week
- 2 to 3 new photos per week
- Review requests after every completed job
- One winter email or text to past customers each week
- Fast callbacks and missed-call texts

That is enough to stay visible while everyone else gets sloppy. A lot of plumbing shops lose winter work because they stop marketing right when demand picks up.

## FAQ

### How often should a plumber post on Google Business Profile in winter?

Two posts per week is a good target during winter. Focus on frozen pipe repair, water heater work, drain cleaning, and emergency service. Keep the posts short, local, and tied to jobs people are actually searching for.

### What winter services should a plumbing company promote?

Frozen pipe repair, water heater replacement, drain cleaning, leak detection, and emergency plumbing usually matter most. In colder markets like Pittsburgh, those services tend to get more urgent once temperatures drop.

### How many Google reviews does a plumber need before winter?

There is no magic number, but a shop with 20 to 30 recent reviews usually looks more trustworthy than one with only a handful. Recency matters too, so keep asking for reviews all season.

### Should plumbers send winter promos by text or email?

Both work, but text gets faster attention. Email is better for longer reminders or a full seasonal offer. The best results usually come from sending a short text first, then following up by email.

Use a winter landing page, mention frozen pipe repair on your Google Business Profile, and send reminders before the first hard freeze. Fast answer times matter too, because frozen pipe customers usually call the first plumber who picks up.
