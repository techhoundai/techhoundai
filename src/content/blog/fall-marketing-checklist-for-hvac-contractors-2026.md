# Fall Marketing Checklist for HVAC Contractors in 2026

September is when homeowners stop ignoring the furnace. The first cold week turns "I'll call later" into "my heat is dead right now." A solid fall marketing checklist for HVAC contractors gets your company in front of those jobs before the scramble starts.

The work is plain and boring, which is why it works. Update Google, contact past customers, tighten the website, and set up fast follow-up. Do that now, and you get more heating calls while other shops are still acting surprised that fall happened.

## Fall marketing checklist for HVAC contractors

The goal is not to post random stuff and hope for the best. The goal is to show up when people search for furnace repair, tune-ups, boiler service, and emergency heat help.

Start with these six moves.

1. Update your Google Business Profile.
2. Refresh furnace and boiler pages on your website.
3. Send a fall promo to past customers.
4. Turn on missed call texts.
5. Ask for reviews after every heating job.
6. Track calls, not likes.

A Pittsburgh HVAC company can do most of this in one afternoon. The fastest money usually comes from Google Business Profile and old-customer outreach. Social media can help, but a pretty post about filters does not pay the gas bill.

## Update your Google Business Profile before the first cold snap

Google Business Profile is still the fastest place to win local heating calls. People search there when the heat quits, and they are not shopping around for fun.

Start by checking the basics. Your business name, phone number, hours, and service area should match your website exactly. Add your heating services, not just broad labels like "HVAC." Use terms people actually type, like furnace repair, furnace installation, boiler repair, heat pump service, and heating maintenance.

Add fresh photos every week in the fall. Post shots from real jobs, not stock photos of smiling people in clean rooms. A blurry furnace room photo beats a fake one, because it proves the business is active.

Also update your hours for weekends, emergency calls, and holiday schedules. A lot of heating leads come in after 4 p.m. when people realize the house is getting cold. If you serve Pittsburgh, McKees Rocks, Bethel Park, Brookline, or Monroeville, make sure the service area is accurate and only list places the truck actually covers.

Weekly Google posts help too. One post can promote a furnace tune-up, a thermostat check, or a safety inspection. That takes 10 minutes and costs nothing.

## Send seasonal offers to past customers

Past customers are the easiest people to sell in the fall. They already know the company, and they already own a heating system that will eventually act up.

Send one email in late September, one in mid-October, and one after the first cold snap. Text can work too, especially for maintenance plan renewals and tune-up reminders. Email is better for longer messages. Text is better when the offer is simple.

Good fall offers are boring on purpose. Think furnace tune-up, safety inspection, filter replacement, thermostat check, or maintenance plan renewal. A discount on labor is fine. Big discounts on repairs usually attract the wrong kind of customer.

Here is a simple example. A contractor sends a fall email to 800 customers. The email platform costs about $30 to $100 a month. A decent campaign might book 10 to 20 tune-ups, plus a few repair calls that turn into bigger work later. That beats waiting for the phone to ring.

Postcards still work too, especially for older customers who ignore email. Printing and mailing can run about $0.60 to $1.20 per card, depending on list size and postage. The trick is to keep the offer short. Nobody wants a brochure about the history of the company.

## Fix the website pages that book heating jobs

A fall campaign falls flat if the website looks like a brochure. People need one clear path to a call or quote request.

The homepage should say what the company does, where it works, and what problem it solves. Put a tap-to-call button near the top. Put it there again lower on the page. On mobile, people should not have to hunt for a phone number like it is hidden in a scavenger hunt.

The website also needs dedicated heating pages. At minimum, build pages for furnace repair, furnace installation, heating maintenance, and emergency HVAC service. Add service area pages for the towns you actually want. A single "services" page is lazy, and Google usually treats it that way.

Each page should answer three things fast. What do you fix? Where do you work? Why should someone call now? Add a few job photos, a short FAQ, and a quote form that does not ask for 19 fields of nonsense.

Speed matters too. A slow site loses calls on mobile, especially when a homeowner is standing in a cold kitchen. If the page takes forever to load, people bounce and call the next company in the map pack.

## Turn missed calls into booked jobs

HVAC is a call-heavy business. That means missed calls are lost money unless something catches them.

Missed call text-back is the simplest fix. When someone calls and nobody answers, the system sends a text in under a minute. It can say, "Sorry we missed your call. Need furnace repair, a tune-up, or emergency heat help?" That one message keeps a lead warm instead of letting it go cold.

The same idea works for estimates and service reminders. Send a quick follow-up after a quote, then another one the next day. Most people do not book because they forgot, not because they hated the price.

Review requests belong here too. Ask for a Google review right after the job, while the customer is still happy and the furnace is running. A short text with a direct link is usually enough. Do not make people search for your listing like they are doing detective work.

Automation tools for this usually cost less than one lost service call. Even a small setup can make a big difference in September and October, when the phones start lighting up.

## Track the numbers that matter through November

A lot of contractors say marketing does not work because they never tracked the right things. Likes are not the right things.

Watch these numbers instead.

- Calls from Google Business Profile.
- Website calls and form fills.
- Quote requests for heating work.
- Reviews collected after jobs.
- Booked tune-ups and repair calls.

Check the numbers every week from September through November. If the website gets traffic but nobody calls, the phone button or service page is weak. If Google Business Profile gets views but no calls, the profile needs better photos, reviews, and service details.

A simple target helps. If 100 website visitors turn into 3 to 5 calls, the site is doing fine. If 100 visitors turn into zero calls, something is broken. Usually it is the page, the offer, or the follow-up.

The fall season is short. Waiting until the first freeze is how shops end up paying extra for every lead.

## Bottom line

The best fall marketing checklist for HVAC contractors is not fancy. Update Google Business Profile, contact past customers, fix the website, and answer leads fast. That is the whole game.
