# How Many Google Reviews Does an HVAC Business Need?

Getting more Google reviews is one of the few marketing jobs that still pays off after the truck leaves the driveway. There is no magic number, but there is a point where an HVAC profile starts looking trusted and another point where it starts looking thin.

For most HVAC companies, 25 reviews is the first real milestone. Around 50 reviews, the business looks established. At 100 reviews, most homeowners stop wondering whether the shop is new.

## How many Google reviews does an HVAC business need?

Short answer: enough to look busy, current, and local. If the competitors in your area have 80 to 120 reviews, sitting at 12 will hurt you more than a half-star difference.

A practical target looks like this:
- New solo shop: 15 to 30 reviews
- Small team with 2 to 5 trucks: 50 to 100 reviews
- Busy company in a metro area: 100 to 200 reviews

Those are not Google rules. They are practical benchmarks from the real world. A 4.8 rating with 62 reviews usually feels stronger than a 5.0 rating with 8 reviews.

In Pittsburgh, that matters in places like Cranberry, Robinson, Mt. Lebanon, and the South Hills. Homeowners compare fast, and they often call the shop that looks active right now.

## Why Google reviews matter for HVAC local SEO

Google says local results come down to relevance, distance, and prominence. Reviews sit inside that last bucket. That means they are not window dressing.

More reviews can help in three ways. First, they improve trust before the click. Second, they can help the profile look more established. Third, they give your business more chances to show up as the better option when nearby competitors are close on maps.

Review count is not the whole story. Review quality matters too. A steady stream of recent, honest reviews usually beats one old burst from three summers ago.

Here is the simple version. If your last review was during the Biden administration, the profile looks stale. Google users notice that stuff faster than owners do.

## What counts as a strong review profile?

A strong HVAC review profile has four things.

1. A decent number of reviews.
2. Recent reviews, not just old ones.
3. A rating that stays above 4.5.
4. Real comments that mention actual work.

A review that says "Great company" helps a little. A review that says "Replaced our failed furnace in Brentwood the same day" helps more. It gives future customers proof that the shop handles the exact job they need.

Recent reviews matter because they show the business is still active. A shop with 90 reviews and none in the last 12 months looks slower than a shop with 38 reviews and a fresh one every week.

## How to get more Google reviews without being weird about it

The best time to ask is right after the job is done and the customer is relieved. That is when the value is fresh in their head. They just got heat back, or the AC is working again, and they are not thinking about anything else.

Use one simple script:
"Glad we got that fixed. If you were happy with the work, would you mind leaving a Google review? I’ll text you the link."

That works better than a long speech. The ask should feel normal, not desperate.

The next step is to make the link dead simple. Send a text. Put it on the invoice. Add a QR code to the truck leave-behind sheet. If a customer has to hunt for your profile, the review probably will not happen.

A follow-up message helps too. Send one reminder 48 hours later. After that, stop. Nobody wants to be chased like an overdue invoice.

## How many reviews should an HVAC company ask for each month?

A good target is 2 to 4 new reviews per month for a small shop. Bigger teams should aim for 4 to 8. If the business runs a lot of installs and service calls, even more is fair game.

The goal is not a giant spike. The goal is steady review velocity. Google and customers both trust a profile that keeps moving.

Think about the math. If a company closes 80 jobs a month and only asks 10 customers, the review pace will be weak. If the office asks every happy customer, the numbers get better fast.

That is why the ask needs to be part of the process. The tech should not be guessing. The office should not be remembering three days later. The system should already be built.

## What to do if you only have 5 to 10 Google reviews

Start by fixing the basics. Make sure the profile is verified, the phone number is right, the service area is filled out, and the website works on a phone. Then start asking every happy customer.

If you only have a handful of reviews, each new one matters more. Ten reviews makes a business look real. Twenty makes it look active. Fifty starts to feel like a shop people can trust.

Also reply to every review. Keep it short and human. Thank the happy customers. Handle the rough ones without getting defensive.

A good reply does not need to sound polished. It just needs to sound like a person who actually does the work.

## A simple 30-day review plan for HVAC companies

Here is a plan that does not require a whole marketing department.

### Week 1
Set up the review link, text template, and QR code. Put the link where techs and office staff can find it fast.

### Week 2
Train every tech on the one-line ask. Make sure they know when to ask, usually after the customer confirms the system works.

### Week 3
Start sending text requests after completed jobs. Send a single reminder two days later if needed.

### Week 4
Reply to every new review and watch which jobs generate the best responses. Installs often get better reviews than emergency calls, because the customer has more time to breathe.

If you do this for a month, you will see the pattern. The businesses that win reviews are not smoother talkers. They are just more consistent.

## Why this matters more in busy markets

A lot of HVAC owners think every city works the same. It does not. In a place like Pittsburgh, the neighborhood game matters. People search by suburb, zip code, and emergency need.

That means a profile with 18 reviews can win in one area and lose badly in another. If nearby competitors have 70 or 100 reviews, your number has to keep climbing. Otherwise, the customer picks the shop that looks safer.

Reviews are not the only thing that matters. But they are one of the fastest trust signals a homeowner sees. That is why they punch above their weight.

## Final thoughts

How many Google reviews does an HVAC business need? Enough to look established, current, and worth calling. For most companies, 25 is the first target, 50 is competitive, and 100 plus starts to separate you from the pack.

The real trick is not chasing a random number. It is building a process that gets one new review after a good job, then another one, then another one. That is boring work. Boring work wins.
