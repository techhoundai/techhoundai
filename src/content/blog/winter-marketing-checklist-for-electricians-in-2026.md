# Winter Marketing Checklist for Electricians in 2026

Winter is when a lot of electricians either stay visible or disappear. In Pittsburgh, one cold snap can trigger breaker issues, generator calls, space-heater overloads, and a pile of rushed phone calls. This winter marketing checklist for electricians covers the simple fixes that help you book more of those jobs.

## Winter marketing checklist for electricians

### 1. Update your Google Business Profile before the first storm

Your Google Business Profile should look active before the weather turns ugly. Fill in your hours, holiday hours, service areas, phone number, website, and service list. Add fresh photos from real jobs, not the same truck shot from last summer.

Post once a week while winter is active. A short update about generator installs, panel upgrades, surge protection, or emergency repair keeps your profile from looking stale. If your profile still looks like it is hibernating, people notice.

### 2. Put winter services on the website homepage

Winter buyers are looking for fast help, not a brand story. Make sure the homepage shows your most profitable winter services first. That usually means emergency electrical repair, panel replacements, generator installs, surge protection, indoor lighting, and EV charger work.

Add separate pages for those services if they do not already exist. A homeowner with a tripping breaker at 8 p.m. does not want to dig through a page about commercial fit-outs. They want one clear path to a call.

Service area pages matter too. If you work in Pittsburgh, Cranberry, Monroeville, Mt. Lebanon, and the North Hills, say so clearly. Search engines and homeowners both like specifics.

### 3. Ask for reviews while the job is fresh

Reviews move the needle in winter because people are scared, tired, and in a hurry. Ask for the review before you leave the job or within 24 hours. The ask should be short and direct.

A simple text works well: Thanks for letting us handle the panel issue today. If you have 30 seconds, a Google review would help a lot. Here is the link.

Aim for a steady rhythm instead of a random burst. Ten review asks a week is a lot better than asking nobody for a month and then panicking in January. Reply to every review, too. That shows activity and gives you another chance to mention the service and city.

### 4. Send a winter email to past customers

A past customer list is one of the easiest winter marketing wins. Pull older customers from your CRM and send a useful email, not a sales essay. Good winter offers for electricians include panel safety checks, generator service, surge protection, outdoor outlet repair, and holiday lighting help.

Send one campaign in early winter and another after the first major cold snap. If your list is big, split it by job type. Customers with older panels or generator installs should get a different message than people who only had a light fixture swapped last year.

A good email has one subject line, one offer, and one button. Do not stuff five services into one message. That just gives people homework, and nobody wants homework from an electrician.

### 5. Set up missed call auto-texts and after-hours follow-up

Winter calls happen early, late, and during bad weather. If nobody answers, the customer usually calls the next name on Google Maps. That is how easy jobs disappear.

Set up an auto-text that replies right away when a call is missed. Keep it short. Tell them you got the message, when they can expect a callback, and what to do if it is an emergency. Then make sure someone actually follows up.

A short voicemail helps too. Say your business name, service area, and normal response window. Skip the long welcome message and the company history. Nobody is listening for your origin story while their breaker keeps tripping.

### 6. Track which winter jobs make money

Not every winter lead is worth the same. Troubleshooting, panel work, generator installs, and safety upgrades usually pay better than tiny one-off fixes. If you know what last winter produced, you can advertise the right thing this winter.

Look at your jobs by type, not just by count. Ten low-margin calls can eat more time than three solid panel jobs. That matters when the schedule is already packed and the weather is miserable.

This is also where local patterns help. Pittsburgh neighborhoods do not all buy the same work at the same pace. Some areas call for emergency repairs first. Others care more about generators, lighting, or upgrade work.

## What to do this week

If the busy season is about to start, do not turn this into a giant project. Fix the profile, update the homepage, ask for reviews, send one email, and test the missed-call text. That is enough to make a real difference before winter gets serious.

A simple seven-day plan looks like this:

- Monday: update Google Business Profile
- Tuesday: add or refresh your main winter service pages
- Wednesday: ask for reviews from recent jobs
- Thursday: send one winter email campaign
- Friday: test your missed call text and voicemail
- Next week: check which calls turned into booked jobs

That is boring work. It also works. The electricians who stay visible in winter usually win because they look active, answer fast, and make it easy to call.

## Final thought

Winter marketing for electricians is not about clever ads or fancy branding. It is about showing up in Google, looking busy, and making it easy to book the next job. A clean profile, a useful website, a few good reviews, and a follow-up system will beat a sleepy competitor more often than not.
