# Best CRM for HVAC Businesses in 2026

The best CRM for HVAC businesses is the one your team will actually use every day. For most shops, that means something simple enough for techs, but strong enough for dispatch, estimates, reminders, and payments.

In 2026, the top options are Jobber, Housecall Pro, FieldPulse, and ServiceTitan. The right pick depends on crew size, budget, and how much office work your shop needs to handle.

## What an HVAC CRM should actually do

A decent HVAC CRM is more than a contact list with a fancy logo. It should help you book jobs, send estimates, follow up on leads, track service history, and keep maintenance customers from slipping through the cracks.

For HVAC specifically, look for these features:

- Scheduling and dispatching
- Estimates and invoices
- Text reminders and follow-ups
- Recurring maintenance agreements
- QuickBooks integration
- Mobile app for techs in the field
- Payment processing
- Reporting for revenue, closed jobs, and tech performance

If a CRM cannot handle reminders or service plans, it will create more admin work than it removes. That is how software turns into a very expensive headache.

For a small HVAC shop in Pittsburgh, the goal is simple. Get the call booked, get the quote sent, get the follow-up out, and get paid. Everything else is decoration.

## Best CRM for HVAC businesses: quick comparison

Here is the short version before we get into the details.

| CRM | Best for | Public starting price | Strengths | Weak spot |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Jobber | Solo techs and small crews | $29/month billed annually | Easy to learn, affordable, strong basics | Gets pricey as the team grows |
| Housecall Pro | Growing HVAC shops | $59/month billed annually | Strong balance of features and ease | Higher tiers add up fast |
| FieldPulse | Shops that want flexible pricing | $39/month | Trade-focused, seat-based pricing, adaptable | Pricing is less transparent |
| ServiceTitan | Larger HVAC companies | Custom quote | Deep dispatch, reporting, and operations tools | Usually too much for small shops |

### My honest take

- **Jobber** is the best entry-level choice.
- **Housecall Pro** is the best middle-ground choice.
- **FieldPulse** is a strong fit if you want flexible pricing.
- **ServiceTitan** is the best heavy-duty platform for bigger HVAC operations.

That does not mean the most expensive one is the best one. A seven-tech HVAC company in Pittsburgh has different needs than a one-truck shop doing furnace tune-ups and AC repairs.

## Jobber is the best CRM for small HVAC businesses

Jobber is usually the easiest recommendation for a small HVAC company. It is simple, clean, and built for service businesses that do not want a 90-minute training session just to send an estimate.

At the time of writing, Jobber’s public pricing starts at $29 per month billed annually for the entry plan. That makes it one of the cheapest serious options for small contractors.

### Why HVAC shops like Jobber

- Easy for office staff to learn
- Good for estimates, invoicing, and scheduling
- Solid mobile app for techs
- Good fit for recurring maintenance work
- Good for smaller teams that need speed, not complexity

### Where Jobber falls short

Jobber is great until the shop gets more complex. Once you have more techs, more dispatching, and more moving parts, you may want deeper workflow tools.

It is not the best choice if you need a very advanced operations setup. It is the best choice if you want your team to stop using paper, text threads, and memory.

### Best fit

Jobber works well for solo HVAC owners, small service teams, and shops that mostly handle residential calls. If your office is just one person and a whiteboard, Jobber probably makes sense.

## Housecall Pro is the best all-around CRM for growing HVAC shops

Housecall Pro is a strong middle-ground option. It gives you more polish than basic tools, but it does not feel as heavy as enterprise software.

Housecall Pro’s public pricing starts at $59 per month billed annually for the Basic plan, with higher tiers for shops that need more features.

### Why growing HVAC companies pick Housecall Pro

- Strong scheduling and dispatch tools
- Easy quote and invoice flow
- Good recurring service tools
- Customer communication tools that keep jobs moving
- Good fit for shops with office staff and field techs

### Where Housecall Pro makes sense

Housecall Pro is a good choice when your HVAC business is past the startup stage. You have real volume, a steady office process, and enough calls that missed follow-up hurts.

That is usually the point where a shop needs more than a simple app. It needs a system.

### Where it can get annoying

Housecall Pro can get expensive as you move up plans. That is normal in software, but it still matters. Always match the plan to the work you actually do.

If your shop is tiny, you may pay for features you never touch. That is a bad habit in business and a bad habit in software buying.

## FieldPulse is a good fit if you want flexible pricing

FieldPulse is often a smart pick for HVAC shops that want trade-specific tools without jumping straight into the biggest platform on the market.

FieldPulse starts at $39 per month publicly, and its pricing model can be easier to scale for some teams. The downside is that you usually need to talk to sales for the full picture.

### Why FieldPulse stands out

- Built for field service contractors
- Flexible structure for growing teams
- Good for shops that want trade-specific workflows
- Strong option for owners who hate bloated software

### What to watch out for

The biggest issue is transparency. Some owners want to see the full price before they sit through a demo. Fair enough.

If you like having someone tailor the setup to your shop, FieldPulse can work well. If you want a buy-it-now experience, it may feel slower than Jobber.

### Best fit

FieldPulse is a good choice for HVAC companies that are growing, but still want a practical setup. It can be a solid middle lane between basic software and enterprise-level systems.

## ServiceTitan is the best CRM for larger HVAC companies

ServiceTitan is the heavyweight in this group. It is built for larger service businesses that need deep scheduling, dispatching, customer tracking, reporting, and operational control.

ServiceTitan does not publish standard pricing. You need a custom quote, which is common for software at this level.

### Why bigger HVAC shops choose ServiceTitan

- Deep dispatch and scheduling tools
- Strong reporting and management visibility
- Membership and recurring revenue support
- Advanced workflow control for larger teams
- Better fit for multi-truck operations with office staff

### Why smaller shops should be careful

ServiceTitan is powerful, but power is not free. The setup can be heavier, the price is higher, and the learning curve is real.

If you run a two-truck HVAC shop, this is usually more system than you need. You do not need a battleship to make a house call.

### Best fit

ServiceTitan is best for established HVAC companies with a larger team, real office support, and a clear need for advanced reporting and dispatch control.

## How to choose the right CRM for your HVAC business

Here is the simplest way to decide.

### Choose Jobber if:

- You are a solo owner or small crew
- You want easy setup
- You care about price
- You mostly need scheduling, quotes, invoicing, and reminders

### Choose Housecall Pro if:

- You are growing fast
- You have office staff and field techs
- You want a balanced set of tools
- You need a stronger all-around system

### Choose FieldPulse if:

- You want a trade-focused setup
- You are okay with a sales quote
- You want flexibility as you grow
- You do not want a giant platform yet

### Choose ServiceTitan if:

- You run a larger HVAC company
- You need advanced dispatch and reporting
- You have multiple techs and office staff
- You want a serious all-in-one system

For a Pittsburgh HVAC company, the right CRM is the one that helps you answer the phone, book the work, and keep the schedule moving when the weather turns ugly. February furnace calls do not wait for bad software to load.

## Common CRM mistakes HVAC owners make

Most bad CRM decisions are not about the software itself. They are about how the software gets chosen.

### 1. Buying too much software

Owners see a long feature list and think bigger is better. Usually, it just means more training and more monthly cost.

### 2. Not setting up follow-up automations

A CRM only helps if it sends reminders, follow-ups, and estimate nudges. If your team still relies on memory, you will still lose leads.

### 3. Ignoring the office workflow

The office staff often lives in the CRM more than the techs do. If the office hates it, the whole system breaks down.

### 4. Skipping migration cleanup

Bad data ruins a good CRM. Clean contacts, service history, and customer notes before you move anything over.

### 5. Forgetting the real goal

The goal is not software. The goal is more booked HVAC jobs, faster follow-up, and less wasted time.

## FAQ

### What is the best CRM for a small HVAC business?

Jobber is usually the best starting point for a small HVAC business. It is easy to learn, affordable, and covers the basics without making your team wrestle with the software all day.

### How much does an HVAC CRM cost?

Public pricing starts around $29 per month for Jobber, $39 per month for FieldPulse, and $59 per month for Housecall Pro. ServiceTitan uses custom pricing, so larger shops need a quote.

### Does Housecall Pro work for HVAC companies?

Yes, Housecall Pro works well for HVAC companies, especially growing shops. It handles scheduling, estimates, invoices, and customer communication well enough for most residential service businesses.

### What should an HVAC CRM include?

It should include scheduling, dispatch, estimates, invoices, reminders, customer history, and a mobile app. If it does not help you book jobs and follow up faster, it is not doing its job.

### How often should an HVAC business send maintenance reminders?

Twice a year is a strong starting point for most HVAC shops, once before cooling season and once before heating season. That keeps tune-ups in front of customers when they actually need them.
